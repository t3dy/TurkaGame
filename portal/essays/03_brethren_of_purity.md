# Ibn Turka and the Brethren of Purity

**The general context.** The *Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ*, the Brethren of Purity, were an
anonymous fraternity of philosophers active in tenth-century Basra whose fifty-two
*Rasāʾil* (Epistles) attempted something no one had attempted in Arabic before: a
complete encyclopedia of the sciences, from arithmetic through music, astronomy,
logic, natural philosophy and metaphysics, unified by a Neoplatonic-Pythagorean
conviction that **number is the ladder by which the soul climbs back toward its
origin**. They wrote anonymously, they wrote systematically, and they wrote for a
reader they intended to transform. Their epistles circulated for centuries and were
read by nearly everyone who mattered — including, by a long chain of transmission,
the compilers of the *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm* (the Latin *Picatrix*).

**The intersection.** Ibn Turka's Cairo circle called itself the Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ.
Not "like" them, not "after" them — *the* Brethren of Purity, the name taken
directly. This is the single most revealing gesture in the whole formation period,
and it is easy to under-read as homage. It is not homage. It is a claim about
unfinished business.

Consider what taking that name commits you to. The old Brethren proposed that the
sciences form one body; Ibn Turka's generation proposed to *complete* the
demonstration by showing that the letters of the Arabic alphabet supply the
mathematical architecture the encyclopedia had gestured at. The old Brethren wrote
anonymously; the new ones were court judges, imperial historians and celebrated
poets whose names were extremely well known — which converts a philosophical
fraternity into something a state can see, and therefore something a state can
prosecute. The old Brethren addressed a reader they hoped to perfect; the New
Brethren addressed patrons who could fund, deploy, and eventually indict them.

**What changes when you read Ibn Turka this way.** The *Investigations* stops
looking like an occult treatise that got out of hand and starts looking like the
missing volume of an encyclopedia — the one where the unifying mathematics is
finally specified rather than promised. And the network Melvin-Koushki calls the
New Brethren stops looking like a coterie and starts looking like what it was: an
attempt to resume a thousand-year-old research program with imperial resources
behind it.

**What the sources will not tell us.** Whether the name was adopted formally or
grew as a nickname; whether the circle had any organizational structure at all
beyond friendship and correspondence; and whether Ibn Turka himself proposed it.
The game treats the naming as a scene the player participates in — an
`ATTESTED` fact (the circle used the name) staged in a `PLAUSIBLE-GAP` moment
(one particular evening, one particular argument).
