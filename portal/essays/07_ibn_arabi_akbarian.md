# Ibn Turka and the Akbarian Inheritance

**The general context.** Ibn ʿArabī (1165–1240), the Andalusian *al-Shaykh
al-Akbar* — the Supreme Master — reorganized Islamic metaphysics around the divine
names. Creation is the self-disclosure of God through His names; the *barzakh*, the
isthmus, is where incompatible orders meet and translate; the imaginal realm is
where meaning takes form. His commentator Qūnavī systematized it; the tradition
became the lingua franca of learned Sufism for centuries.

**The intersection.** Ibn Turka is an Akbarian, and this is the least surprising
thing about him. What is surprising is what he does with the inheritance, and whom
he pairs it with.

First, the transformation: Ibn ʿArabī's divine names are objects of *contemplation*
— you realize them, you are transformed by them. Ibn Turka treats the same
structure as an object of *computation*. If the names and letters really are the
architecture of creation, then their numerical values are not devotional aids but
data, and the relations among them are demonstrable rather than merely disclosed.
This is the move that converts a mystical metaphysics into a claimed universal
science, and it is why the *Investigations* reads like philosophy rather than like
a Sufi manual.

Second, the pairing. Ibn Turka is credited as the first to name **Ibn ʿArabī and
Saʿd al-Dīn Ḥamūya together** as co-founders of the science of letters — the
Andalusian master and his "Iranian twin." To insist on both is to refuse a choice
that partisans on either side wanted made, and to construct a genealogy in which
the Persianate line is not derivative. In the Career Sim this is a Cairo-phase
decision with consequences that reach the tribunals: a scholar who credited his
lineage generously has a defense a scholar who absorbed it silently does not.

**The stakes of a footnote.** It is worth registering how much depends on
something as dry as source-attribution. A genealogy is a claim about who owns a
science. Ibn Turka's version makes lettrism jointly Andalusian and Iranian,
philosophical and mystical, contemplative and operative — which is exactly the
synthesis his own work needs in order to be legitimate.
