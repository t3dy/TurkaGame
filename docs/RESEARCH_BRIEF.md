# Research Brief: Ibn Turka and the Islamicate Occult Courts

Synthesized from the three Matthew Melvin-Koushki papers currently in hand (see
[Sources](#sources) below). This is original paraphrase and synthesis for game-design
purposes, not a reproduction of the source texts — go to the papers themselves
(`research/library/`, gitignored) for the actual scholarship, arguments, and translations.

## Who is Ibn Turka

Ṣāʾin al-Dīn ʿAlī ibn Turka Iṣfahānī (1369–1432) was Chief Judge (*qāḍī*) of Isfahan and,
per Melvin-Koushki, the foremost occult philosopher of Timurid Iran. His day job was law;
his life's project, developed alongside it, was a mathematical-theurgic philosophy —
consciously Pythagorean, consciously modern — that challenged the dominant
Aristotelian-Avicennan philosophical system of his day.

His masterwork, *Investigations* (*Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ*, 1420), is described as the first
fully systematic summa of Islamic lettrism (*ʿilm al-ḥurūf*, often glossed as "Islamic
kabbalah") — a science treating language, especially Arabic letters and their numeric
values, as the mathematical substrate of the cosmos itself.

Melvin-Koushki's framing: Ibn Turka is worth reading alongside Nicholas of Cusa, Pico,
Bruno, and John Dee as a comparable figure of the "Scientific Revolution" story — a
mathematizing, theurgic, empire-scaled system-builder — except he had far greater
*immediate* political and scientific impact than any of his Latin peers. His cosmology
became, in Melvin-Koushki's words, something like source code for Timurid and then
Safavid, Aqquyunlu, Uzbek, Ottoman, and Mughal imperial self-conception — a
"post-Mongol Occult-Scientific Revolution" spanning the whole Persianate world.

## Biography — the game-relevant beats

- **Mentor:** Sayyid Ḥusayn Akhlāṭī (d. 1397), a Cairo-based lettrist, alchemist, and
  geomancer, hub of a network Ibn Turka and colleagues called the "New Brethren of
  Purity" (consciously invoking the medieval Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ), radiating from Cairo to
  Iran, India, and Anatolia.
- **Best friend and foremost student:** Sharaf al-Dīn ʿAlī Yazdī (d. 1454) — Timurid
  imperial historian and mathematician, worked at the Samarkand Observatory, and (per
  Melvin-Koushki) the first to fully "astrolettrize" the writing of history itself.
- **Patrons:** first Iskandar Sultan (a Timurid prince whose atelier was known for fusing
  astronomy, poetry, and manuscript painting), then Bāysunghur (a Timurid prince and
  accomplished calligrapher, likely commissioner of the monumental Bāysunghur Qurʾan).
- **Samarkand Observatory:** Ibn Turka's *Investigations* was composed in 1420, the same
  year Ulugh Beg's observatory was under construction; its star tables were the most
  accurate produced before the telescope and remained in use in London into the 19th
  century. Melvin-Koushki treats the coincidence as more than decorative — star science
  and letter science boomed together in this milieu.
- **Three inquisitions:** Ibn Turka, known as a judge who defended the weak against the
  powerful, was subjected to three separate state inquisitions engineered by rival,
  jealous colleagues. He successfully defended himself the first two times. The third
  time he did not — five years of wandering exile followed, and he died in 1432,
  impoverished, in legal limbo.
- A Cairo companion, the Sufi poet Qāsim-i Anvār (d. 1434), suffered a parallel fate —
  exiled in 1427 over the same lettrist associations.

This is a ready-made three-act structure: rising scholar under a great mentor → court
success across two successive royal patrons → catastrophic third accusation, exile, and
a death that (per Melvin-Koushki, drawing the parallel explicitly) only later
scholarship vindicates — the same narrative shape as Giordano Bruno's.

## The occult sciences as a structured hierarchy

Two distinct hierarchies turn up in the sources, and they map cleanly onto game systems.

**1. The "Occult Quintet" (*khamsa-yi muḥtajiba*)** — five canonical occult sciences,
named via the mnemonic *kullu-hu sirr* ("the whole is a secret," letters K-L-H-S-R),
documented in the Kāshifī/ʿAlī Ṣafī textual tradition that Ibn Turka's circle fed into.
They run in explicit descending order of technical difficulty, cost, and elite status:

1. **kīmiyā** — alchemy: elevating base metals to silver or gold.
2. **līmiyā** — talismanry: conjoining active celestial forces with passive terrestrial
   ones to produce effects.
3. **hīmiyā** — subjugation: persuading planets and compelling jinn through rigorous
   ritual, amuletic, or invocatory practice.
4. **sīmiyā** — illusionism: projecting convincing but unreal images into observers'
   perception.
5. **rīmiyā** — trickery: mixing terrestrial substances to produce startling effects.

This is an already-authored skill-tree spine — five branches, explicitly ordered from
"most prestigious/expensive/hard to learn" to "cheapest/most crowd-pleasing," with real
period vocabulary attached.

**2. The seven-tier epistemic hierarchy**, from Ibn Turka's own treatise *On the
Splitting of the Moon and the Last Hour*: all schools of thought arranged from
"traditionist literalism" at the bottom to "lettrism" at the top, as the apex of human
perfection. Our current sources only confirm the two endpoints plus three named
intermediate groups — theologians, philosophers, and Sufis, in ascending order — not the
full seven. **This is an open research gap**: the complete breakdown is cited to a
different Melvin-Koushki article, "Selenocentrism and Heliocentrism," not yet in hand.
Don't invent the missing tiers; go get the source first (see
[docs/DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md)).

## Cosmology and core concepts (flavor + mechanics material)

- **Lettrism (*ʿilm al-ḥurūf*):** Arabic letters, especially the Qurʾan's "mysterious
  letters" (*muqaṭṭaʿāt*) opening certain suras, are treated as mathematical-cosmic units
  carrying numeric value (*abjad*) that can be manipulated (e.g. *taksīr*, a
  letter-decomposition technique) to reveal hidden meaning. Ibn Turka's own epigraph:
  *the more a thing is occulted, the more it is made manifest.*
- **The Ṭahawī Circle** — Ibn Turka's central diagram in *Investigations*, described as
  the first "modern" lettrist reworking of the Pythagorean Tetractys. A real,
  manuscript-attested diagram (Tehran, Majlis Library MS 10196, f. 63a) — a strong first
  candidate for a curated visual asset.
- **Planet–Pearl–Peach** — the *Mafāḥiṣ*'s own structure: three "Globes of Light"
  corresponding to Mental, Written, and Spoken registers, organized as an
  ascent-descent-ascent journey. Good scaffolding for a VN act structure or a roguelike
  "three worlds" layout.
- **Talismanic architecture:** Safavid New Isfahan and the Shaykh Luṭf Allāh Mosque are
  read as literal built diagrams of this cosmology — cities and buildings as
  functioning talismans, not just symbolic ones.
- **"New Brethren of Purity"** — the self-chosen name of Ibn Turka's court-occultist
  circle, explicitly invoking the medieval Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ. A natural in-world faction
  name.

## Courts and political economy (career-sim material)

- Occult science functioned as literal political technology across the entire
  post-Mongol Persianate world — Timurid, Aqquyunlu, Safavid, Uzbek, Ottoman, and Mughal
  courts all adopted versions of the same Pythagorean-astrological-lettrist platform as
  imperial cosmology.
- **Patronage was transactional and explicit:** works were literally commissioned as
  "royal boons" — e.g. *Boon for the Khan*, dedicated to a specific Safavid Qizilbash
  (Turkic military-elite) patron. What got written, and how openly, tracked what the
  patron wanted popularized versus kept elite/secret.
- **Popularization was a live tension, not a given:** Ibn Turka's own style is
  deliberately dense and "ontologically ornate," aimed at an elite readership, in
  contrast to explicit "Everyman's Library" projects by Ḥusayn Vāʿiẓ Kāshifī and his son
  ʿAlī Ṣafī, who simplified and popularized the same material for wider, more commercial
  circulation. That's a legible game axis: hoard prestige/secrecy vs. teach for
  reach/income.
- **Real institutional risk:** political and religious rivals could and did trigger
  state inquisitions against a scholar; patron favor and public reputation were
  load-bearing resources that could be spent down. Ibn Turka survived two attacks and
  lost the third.
- **Institutions to model:** royal ateliers/*kitābkhānas* (combined book-production and
  art workshops — Iskandar Sultan's and Bāysunghur's are both named), observatories
  (Samarkand), the judiciary (Ibn Turka's actual day job — occult science was his second
  career, layered on top of, not instead of, formal legal office), Sufi orders
  (Naqshbandī, Niʿmatallāhī both named in the sources), and the military-patron class
  (Qizilbash).

## Sample "spellbook" texture

*Boon for the Khan*, a Persian manual of illusionism and trickery in the same textual
tradition (translated in full in the "Occult Court" paper), catalogs concrete named
"operations" (*nāmūs*, rite): putrefactions, suffumigations (smoke-based illusions),
food/drink endurance rites, sleeper interrogation, treasure dowsing, predator taming,
instant agriculture, and more. These read as ready-made ability/spell flavor text — real
period texture rather than invented fantasy. **Caution:** the specific English wording is
Melvin-Koushki's own translation and is his copyrighted scholarly work; if game text
draws on these, paraphrase substantially rather than quoting the translation at length,
and cite the source.

## Sources

1. Melvin-Koushki, Matthew. "Dr Dee's Ottoman Adventure." *Hellebore*, Fall 2021, 71–79.
   A comparative counterfactual (what if John Dee had sought patronage at the Ottoman
   court instead of failing in England?) that explicitly pairs Dee with Ibn Turka as
   "Timurid Dr. Dee" / "Elizabethan Dr. Littleturk." Useful for the visual novel's
   cross-cultural framing and for period flavor on Ottoman occult-courtly culture
   (Sultan Murad III as astrologer-king, angel-talk, the *Book of Soyga*).
2. Melvin-Koushki, Matthew. "Prologue to Pythagorean Renaissance: Ibn Turka's
   *Investigations* (1420) as Opening Anthem of the Scientific Revolution, Islamic and
   Otherwise." Core biographical and cosmological source — see above.
3. Melvin-Koushki, Matthew. "The Occult Court." *Al-Masaq: Journal of the Medieval
   Mediterranean*, 2025. Core source for court culture, patronage economics, and the
   Occult Quintet hierarchy; includes a full translated excerpt of ʿAlī Ṣafī's *Boon for
   the Khan*.
