# Visionary Environments

*Written 2026-08-31, out of the [Yūsuf Ascent](../games/yusuf-ascent/README.md) build.*

The Bihzād folio turned out to survive being taken apart and walked through. This
document asks which **other** images from Ibn Turka's world would survive the same
treatment, and what each one would become if it did — with a bias toward images that
carry a *mechanism* we can build, not just an atmosphere we can imitate.

---

## First, a correction to the vocabulary

Ted's note on the source file reads the Bihzād as "a psychedelic out-of-body philosophy,"
and that is a good instinct about the picture. But *psychedelic* is a twentieth-century
word for a twentieth-century experience, and if we build to it we will end up making
lava-lamp Orientalism.

The period has its own precise vocabulary for exactly this, and our corpus supports it
with page references:

- **ʿālam al-khayāl** — the imaginal realm: where forms subsist *as images*, neither
  abstract nor material. Kāshifī defines *sīmiyā* as the manipulation of imaginal
  constructs (*khayālāt*) (Melvin-Koushki, *Pseudo-Shaykh Bahāʾī*, 15).
- **barzakh** — the isthmus. Whatever emerges from the occult into the manifest acquires
  an imaginal likeness that acts as a joining isthmus between the angelic realm and the
  body (*Occult Ecumenism*, 24).
- **miʿrāj** — ascent, the primary mode of prophecy, with Muḥammad's own flight as the
  exemplar (*Mir Damad's On Doffing*, 9).
- **khalʿ** — doffing the body. One who can doff the body and reject the senses ascends;
  on this account one is not a philosopher until able to (ibid., 15). Suhrawardī adds
  that the path is *necessarily traumatic* and names talismans as engines of ascent
  (ibid., 14).

**Design consequence.** A visionary environment in this project is not a dream sequence
with warped colours. It is a **technique with a cost, performed deliberately, that moves
you between ontological levels.** Trauma is part of the account, so a clean reward
misrepresents the sources. Every proposal below is built on that.

One more corpus finding worth having in front of us: **paintings of the Prophet's ascent
began to appear as manuscript frontispieces within a few years of Ibn Turka's death, and
became a standard Safavid, Mughal and Ottoman idiom** (*Prologue to a Pythagorean
Renaissance*, 10). That is the single strongest bridge our corpus offers between Ibn
Turka's moment and Persianate visionary painting. It is chronological adjacency and shared
milieu — not evidence of influence — and should be described that way in anything we ship.

## The candidates

Ranked by a blunt criterion: **does the image carry a mechanic, or only a mood?**

### 1. The Timurid *Miʿrājnāma* — Herat, c. 1436 · BnF, Suppl. turc 190

**The single most important comparandum this project has, and it is not close.**

Made in Herat for **Shāh Rukh** — Ibn Turka's own patron, four years after his death — it
holds the most complete cycle of ascension paintings in Islamic art: the Prophet's passage
through the heavens, his encounters with prophets and angels, his colloquy with God, and
visits to the heavenly and infernal realms. Its iconography carries visible Central Asian
and Chinese Buddhist inheritance, which is itself a subject.

- **Why it beats the Bihzād as a game source:** the Bihzād *implies* a ladder and we had to
  argue for it. The Miʿrājnāma *is* a ladder, explicitly, in sequence, with named
  inhabitants at each level. Everything we had to infer, it states.
- **Rights lead:** BnF, digitised. Gallica's terms for pre-1500 manuscript reproductions
  are usually permissive; check the specific item.
- **Reading:** Christiane Gruber, *The Timurid Book of Ascension* (2008).

> **Environment — "The Seven Heavens."** A room-per-heaven crawl where each level has a
> **gatekeeper who asks for a credential**, not a key. What you carry up is your accumulated
> standing (a lettrist term, a patron's endorsement, a text you have actually read), and
> some levels refuse you for reasons that are *social* rather than puzzle-shaped — which is
> the real texture of Ibn Turka's career. The descent is mandatory and costs something.
> This is the direct sequel to Yūsuf Ascent's Prototype B, and the one to build next.

### 2. Nizami's *Haft Paykar* — the seven pavilions

Bahrām Gūr's architect builds seven domed pavilions coloured for the seven planets that
rule the seven climes; the king moves through them from the black dome (Saturn) to the
white (Venus), hearing a story in each and being transformed by the sequence. Nizami works
from the music of the spheres via Ptolemy; colour, day of the week, planet and clime are
locked together.

- **Why it is unusually gameable:** it is a *finished level design*. Seven rooms, a fixed
  order, a colour key, a planetary rule per room, and a stated character arc from worldly
  to wise. It is also the exact structural sibling of the seven-rung ladder we built for
  the Bihzād, but with the correspondences supplied by the poet instead of inferred by us.
- **The lettrist hook:** planet-colour-clime correspondence is precisely the kind of
  correspondence table Ibn Turka's science runs on. A *Haft Paykar* environment is where
  the game's astrological and lettrist systems could be taught without a tutorial.
- **Rights lead:** many illustrated Khamsa copies; Harvard Art Museums and the Freer/Sackler
  hold Black Pavilion folios with open-access images.

> **Environment — "Seven Domes."** Seven rooms, each a single dominant hue, each governed by
> one planet's rules for what actions succeed. The player learns the correspondence table by
> being punished by it, then can predict the seventh room before entering.

### 3. Muhammad Siyah Qalam — Topkapı albums H.2153 / H.2160

Late fifteenth-century, probably Tabriz under the Aqquyunlu: wrinkled figures in heavy
garments, dark-skinned figures, and demons, in black line (*siyāh qalam*) with wash, by
several hands under one later attribution. Debated for a century — nomads or demons,
observation or vision. It has been suggested the pictures belonged to a **professional
storyteller** who held them up at the crucial moment of a tale.

- **The mechanism, and it is remarkable:** these figures often stand on **nothing**. No
  ground line, no architecture, no horizon — beings on blank paper. Where the Bihzād gives
  a building too coherent in detail and incoherent in space, Siyah Qalam removes space
  altogether and keeps only the bodies.
- **Rights lead:** Topkapı Palace Museum. Needs real checking; not assume-able.
- **Honesty flag:** the Sufi-versus-demon reading is genuinely contested. Any game using
  them should present the debate rather than pick a side.

> **Environment — "The Ground That Isn't."** A scene with no floor and no sky: entities on
> cream paper, positioned only relative to each other. Movement is by **relation**, not
> coordinates — you approach a figure by establishing a relation to it (address, barter,
> threaten, recognise), and the composition rearranges. The storyteller reading gives the
> frame: an unreliable narrator holds up the next picture and you play what he says.

### 4. Qazwīnī's *ʿAjāʾib al-makhlūqāt* — the illustrated cosmography

The *Wonders of Creation*: firmament and its angels, spheres and elements, meteorology,
seas and their monsters, mountains, minerals, plants, animals, and a closing section on
monsters and angels. Illustrated copies were commissioned by Timurid, Safavid and Mughal
rulers; one Timurid-period copy runs to 253 paintings.

- **Why it works:** it is a **complete world model with an index**. Every creature and
  sphere already has an entry, a picture and a place in a hierarchy. It is closer to a game
  database than to a book.
- **Rights lead:** many copies, several with open-access digitisation (Harvard Art Museums;
  Library of Congress).

> **Environment — "The Bestiary Crawl."** Not a level: a **traversable index**. The player
> moves through the book's own hierarchy — supra-terrestrial down to terrestrial — and each
> entry is a room. The gameplay is classification: given a creature, place it correctly in
> the hierarchy, which is exactly the epistemic act the book performs and exactly what our
> Yūsuf Prototype C already prototypes with tiles.

### 5. The Freer *Dīwān* of Sultan Ahmad Jalāyir (d. 1410)

Some of the earliest and finest ink drawings from the Islamic world. The marginal paintings
on eight leaves have absorbed a century of scholarly attention: tinted drawings that **grow
out from under the calligraphy and into the margins**, and that do not illustrate their
nearest verses so much as evoke the soul's quest.

- **Why it matters here:** Sultan Ahmad ruled 1382–1410 — Ibn Turka's youth and Cairo years.
  This is his contemporary visual world, not a later one, which none of the other candidates
  can claim.
- **The mechanism:** the *margin* is the pictorial space, and the text is the terrain the
  images crawl out from under. That is a spatial idea nobody has made a game out of.
- **Rights lead:** Freer Gallery / National Museum of Asian Art, open access.

> **Environment — "The Margin."** The page is the level. The written block is impassable
> terrain; play happens in the margins and gutters, and drawings emerge from *under* the
> text where you disturb it. Reading a line opens the ground beneath it. This is the most
> formally original idea in this document and the cheapest to prototype — it is 2D, it is
> line art, and the existing `imagelab` cutting pipeline handles it directly.

### 6. The *Falnāma* — Book of Omens

Sixteenth- and early-seventeenth-century Iran and Turkey. Monumental volumes with bold
compositions and supersaturated colour, made for courtly use; smaller versions circulated
in the markets of Isfahan and Istanbul, where **fortune-tellers improvised divinations for
paying customers** from the images.

- **Why it is the best pure mechanic in the list:** the image *is* the oracle. The
  interpretation is improvised aloud by a professional for a fee. That is a complete game
  loop — draw, interpret, be paid or not — and it maps directly onto a live seam in this
  project's corpus, which contains four separate Melvin-Koushki studies of geomancy as a
  practised, paid, court-relevant divinatory science.
- **Honesty flag:** it post-dates Ibn Turka by a century and more. Use it as a *mechanic
  source*, and do not put a Falnāma in his hands.
- **Rights lead:** dispersed *Falnāma* folios are spread across Topkapı, the Met, Chester
  Beatty, the Louvre and the Freer. Individual checks required; some are open access.

> **Environment — "The Improviser."** You are the one holding the book. A client arrives with
> a question, you turn up an image, and you must **build a reading out of what is actually in
> the picture** — selecting elements, as in Yūsuf Prototype A — that satisfies the client
> without lying in a way the story will later catch. Reputation is the currency. This is the
> minigame the career sim most obviously lacks.

### 7. The album (*muraqqaʿ*) and its preface

The one candidate with a **cited** link to Ibn Turka rather than an inferred one: our corpus
states that a new Safavid literary genre, the album preface, *explicitly embodied Ibn Turka's
ontogrammatology* (*Prologue to a Pythagorean Renaissance*, 10). See also David Roxburgh,
*The Persian Album, 1400–1600*.

> **Environment — "The Album."** Not a place — a **construction game**. You assemble a
> muraqqaʿ from loose material: paintings, calligraphic specimens, marginalia, other people's
> pages. The preface you write states the theory that makes the sequence coherent, and the
> game scores whether your arrangement actually obeys the theory you claimed. Ibn Turka's
> ontogrammatology becomes a *rule-set the player writes and is then held to*, which is the
> most faithful possible translation of his project into a mechanic.

### 8. The lettrist diagram itself

Magic squares, *būduh*, letter-number grids, the geomantic tableau. Not "illustrations" at
all — operative diagrams, from our own corpus, unambiguously Ibn Turka's own material and
already partly modelled in this repo's other prototypes.

> **Environment — "The Grid Is The Room."** Level geometry generated *from* a magic square:
> cell values determine what a room contains and which moves are legal; the square's own
> constant is the win condition. The player is not solving a puzzle placed in a world; the
> puzzle's structure *is* the world. Closest to the Bihzād insight — that the picture is a
> diagram — of anything on this list.

## The transferable pipeline

Yūsuf Ascent produced a reusable method, and this is the part that generalises:

```
research inbox/images/<file>
        ↓  visual inspection, box-drawing
imagelab/data/regions.json          region boxes, single-sourced
        ↓  imagelab/scripts/cut_regions.py
imagelab/output/<image>/*.png       one sprite per element
        ↓  a per-game build script + hand-authored annotations
games/<game>/data/palace.json       nodes: box, rung, role, card, lock
        ↓
        ├── 2D hotspot prototype    "look"
        ├── 3D stratified prototype "move"
        ├── sort/diagram prototype  "argue"
        └── research portal         every claim grounding-tagged
```

Any of candidates 1–6 can enter at the top of that pipe today. The expensive part is not
the code — it is **the annotation**, and the annotation is research.

Two rules learned the hard way this session, worth carrying forward:

1. **Keep the boxes in `imagelab`, not in the game.** The game's build script merges boxes
   with interpretation. That kept the crops re-cuttable when the reading changed.
2. **Put the interpretation where the player is making the judgement**, not in an About
   page. Every rung assignment in Yūsuf Ascent is labelled as an argument at the point of
   use, and it is the difference between a research tool and a research-themed one.

## Ibn Turka's visionary life: what we can and cannot say

Before any of this is written into his biography, the state of the evidence:

**What the corpus supports.**
- Ascent (*miʿrāj*) as the primary mode of prophecy, and the philosophical ascent modelled
  on it, are central to the tradition he writes in.
- Doffing the body (*khalʿ*) is a named technique with a stated difficulty, and the ability
  to perform it is treated as a threshold for being counted a philosopher at all.
- The imaginal realm is a technical domain with operators — *sīmiyā* is defined as the
  manipulation of *khayālāt*.
- Ibn Turka's own contribution is a **correction**: against Suhrawardī's ascent-only
  sensorium, he demands descent (*tanzīl*) as well, for a total existential system
  (*Pythagorean Sensorium*, 2). **Any visionary environment we build for him should require
  coming back down.** That is his actual signature and it is a game mechanic.
- 25 uses of "dream" and 16 of "ascent" appear in the Yale dissertation alone; it is the
  richest unmined seam we have for this material.

**What it does not support.**
- No attested vision narrative of his own that this project has located.
- No connection between him and Bihzād, Zulaykha, or any of the images above, beyond
  shared milieu and — for the Miʿrājnāma and the Jalāyirid *Dīwān* — shared patrons and
  overlapping decades.

**The honest construction.** Build the visionary life as *practice*, not as *anecdote*.
We can show him attempting a technique the sources describe in detail, at a cost the sources
specify, using a vocabulary the sources supply — without inventing a vision he is not
recorded as having had. That is the same discipline as
[`games/visual-novel/WRITING_GUIDE.md`](../games/visual-novel/WRITING_GUIDE.md), applied to
a different faculty.

## Recommended order

| | Build | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Margin** (Freer *Dīwān*) | Cheapest to prototype, formally the most original, and the only source that is Ibn Turka's actual contemporary visual world. 2D, open-access images, existing pipeline. |
| 2 | **The Improviser** (*Falnāma*) | The mechanic the career sim most visibly lacks, and it plugs straight into the geomancy material we already hold four studies of. |
| 3 | **The Seven Heavens** (*Miʿrājnāma*) | The direct sequel to Yūsuf Ascent, and the strongest source. Deferred to third only because it is the most expensive. |
| 4 | **Seven Domes** (*Haft Paykar*) | Best vehicle for teaching the correspondence tables the whole project runs on. |
| 5 | **The Album** (*muraqqaʿ*) | The only cited Ibn Turka link, and the most faithful translation of his philosophy into a rule-set. Needs the most design work first. |
| — | Siyah Qalam, Qazwīnī, the lettrist grid | Real, and worth doing; blocked behind rights checks (Topkapı) or behind other prototypes proving the pattern. |
