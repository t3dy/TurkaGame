Glossary

The technical vocabulary these pages run on, in one place. Each term links to the entry that treats it properly. Terms are given as the sources give them — transliterated Arabic and Persian, with the diacritics that distinguish them.

ʿilm al-ḥurūf
The science of letters. Ibn Turka's discipline, and on his account the only truly universal science. →
abjad
The assignment of numerical values to the twenty-eight Arabic letters, so that any word has a sum. →
walāya
Sainthood; the authority that comes of proximity to God. The occult sciences were reclassified as sciences of walāya. →
barzakh
The isthmus. The intermediate realm between spirit and matter, and where causation is held to operate. →
ʿālam al-khayāl
The imaginal realm — a domain that is real and perceived, not imagined in the modern sense. →
muqaṭṭaʿāt
The fourteen isolated letters opening twenty-nine Sūras — exactly half the alphabet, and the light (nūrānī) letters. →
nūrānī / ẓulmānī
Light and dark letters. B is first of the dark ones and fountainhead of duality. →
iḥṣāʾī / kitābī / kalāmī
The letter as counted, written and spoken — three of the four registers of the Mafāḥiṣ's analysis. →
fī anfusi-hā
The letters 'as they are in themselves' — the fourth register, and where the claim to universality lives. →
jafr
Letter divination, and the political branch of lettrism. Its inventor is held to be ʿAlī. →
ʿilm al-raml
Geomancy, the science of sand: sixteen figures generated from random marks and read by rule. →
ʿulūm gharība
The 'strange sciences' — the occult sciences, and a term that began as a marker of novelty. →
taḥqīq / taqlīd
Verification against imitation: working a question to its ground, or accepting it on authority. →
awfāq
Magic squares; letter-and-number grids arranged so that every line sums alike. →
iʿjāz
The miraculous inimitability of the Qur'an — the orthodox doctrine Ibn Turka's science claims to demonstrate. →
dhawq
'Tasting' — direct experiential knowledge, admitted as genuine knowledge by Ghazālī. →
yasa
Mongol customary law, abrogated by Shāhrukh in 813/1411 in favour of the sharīʿa. →
qāḍī
Judge. Ibn Turka was Shāfiʿī chief judge of Isfahan and Yazd, a hereditary family office. →
majlis
A session or assembly — a teaching circle, a court audience, or a literary gathering. →
munshaʾāt
A collected correspondence. Ibn Turka's is the richest biographical source for him. →
kitābkhāna
A book-workshop: calligraphers, illuminators, painters and binders under one roof. →
zīj
A set of astronomical tables. Ulugh Beg's remained the world's most accurate for two centuries. →
