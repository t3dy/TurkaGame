ʿIlm al-raml (geomancy) and Ibn Khaldūn's critique of the occult sciences

Ibn Turka, Yazdī, and the Defense of Divination

The general context. ʿIlm al-raml, "the science of the sand," is divination by sixteen figures generated from marks made at random and combined by strict rules. It is cheap, portable, fast to learn, and enormously popular — which is precisely why learned men sneered at it. Ibn Khaldūn (d. 1406), in the Muqaddima, delivered the most influential critique of the occult sciences ever written in Arabic, and geomancy came in for particular contempt.

The intersection. Sharaf al-Dīn ʿAlī Yazdī — Ibn Turka's closest friend, the man he called his brother in God, later imperial historian and a working mathematician at the Samarkand observatory — wrote a rebuttal. Melvin-Koushki's study of it is titled, precisely, In Defense of Geomancy: Sharaf al-Dīn Yazdī Rebuts Ibn Khaldūn's Critique of Occult Science.

That single fact rearranges the field. The defense of divination in this milieu does not come from a credulous fringe; it comes from the observatory, from a man who computed star tables for a living and wrote the dynasty's official history. The argument is not "wonders are possible." It is a methodological argument about what counts as a science, made by someone with standing to make it.

Ibn Turka's own formation includes the same current: his Cairo master, Sayyid Ḥusayn Akhlāṭī, was a lettrist, alchemist and geomancer, and the sand-figures are one of the doors the Career Sim opens in his first scene.

The epistemological register. This is the part most easily lost. These men were not committed to the proposition that anything is possible. The later manuals explicitly exclude operations judged impracticable — instantaneous travel, shapeshifting, invisibility — while retaining what could be demonstrated. That is a working demarcation criterion, arrived at from inside the tradition. Both games in this project therefore give the player a credulity-versus-demonstration axis: investigate the claim, reproduce it, expose it, reinterpret it, or refuse to judge. Choosing what counts as knowledge is the deepest move available.
