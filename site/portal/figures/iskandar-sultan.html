Iskandar Sulṭān

Iskandar Sulṭān (1384–1415), Temür's grandson and ruler of Fars — Ibn Turka's first and most consequential patron. He commissioned Of Letters, and it was almost certainly at his instance that Ibn Turka began the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Book of Inquiries]]. His court was famous for astrological and occult patronage on a scale that set a model; his defeat in 1415 removed Ibn Turka's protection at a stroke.

Iskandar Sulṭān b. ʿUmar Shaykh (1384–1415) ruled Fars from Shiraz and Isfahan as one of the most lavishly bookish and most astrologically preoccupied of the Timurid princes. His patronage is directly responsible for Ibn Turka's two central lettrist works: he commissioned Of Letters, and Melvin-Koushki judges that Ibn Turka began the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ]] shortly afterward, again almost certainly at Iskandar's instance (Of Islamic Grammatology, 66).

The relationship matters beyond biography. Melvin-Koushki names the model established by Iskandar Sulṭān and [[ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] as what ensured astronomy-astrology and lettrism in particular would go on being heavily patronised by later Timurid, Indo-Timurid, Safavid and Ottoman courts (Philosopher-Kings as Sultan-Scientists, 599). Iskandar is the opening term of the pattern that defines the [[isfahan-circle|occult court]].

He rebelled against Shāhrukh and was defeated, blinded and killed in 1415. For Ibn Turka the consequence was immediate and lasting: the patron who had commissioned his life's work became, retroactively, a dangerous association — the first of the reversals that would culminate in his exile from Herat in 1427.
