Mīr Dāmād

Mīr Dāmād (d. 1631), Twelver Shiʿi philosopher of Safavid Isfahan and — on Melvin-Koushki's argument — the man largely responsible for the neopythagoreanization of Safavid philosophy. He did it by adopting a particular brand of Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu philosophical [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] in at least three of his works. He is the documented link by which Ibn Turka's science reaches the seventeenth century, and the reason the tradition's chief opponent ends up inside it.

Why this entry exists

The portal needs a Safavid successor, and for a long time the obvious candidate was [[mulla-sadra|Mullā Ṣadrā]]. The corpus does not support that: Ṣadrā appears twice in forty-three sources. Mīr Dāmād appears 135 times, and one article — World as (Arabic) Text — is substantially about him. He is the figure the scholarship actually puts in this position.

The argument he is used to make

Melvin-Koushki's claim about Safavid philosophy has two halves, and the second is the contentious one.

The Neoplatonic and perennialist-antiquarian character of Safavid philosophy is, he grants, widely recognised by specialists. But few have admitted its equally remarkable neopythagorean turn — and the principal mode in which that neoplatonic-neopythagorean philosophy was lived as a Safavid imperial way of life, namely occult science, has been completely ignored, which makes a history of its practice impossible (World as (Arabic) Text, résumé).

That is a strong charge against a field: not that it has the emphasis wrong but that it has omitted the thing that would let the subject be studied at all.

What he actually did

Mīr Dāmād is described as a Twelver Shiʿi sage-mage, known until recently as an occult scientist, and he is held largely responsible for the neopythagoreanization of Safavid philosophy — accomplished through his adherence to a particular brand of Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu philosophical lettrism (ʿilm al-ḥurūf) in at least three of his many works (World as (Arabic) Text, résumé).

The hyphenated chain in that sentence is the whole transmission history compressed: Mamluk (Cairo, al-Būnī, [[sayyid-husayn-akhlati|Akhlāṭī]]), Timurid (Ibn Turka and the [[isfahan-circle|Isfahan Circle]]), Aqquyunlu (the Turkman courts between), arriving in Safavid Isfahan. Mīr Dāmād is the terminus, and what he inherited is identified by period rather than by person — a lineage, not a citation.

The transmogrification of Ibn Sīnā

The most striking consequence, and the portal's favourite piece of irony: the neopythagoreanization Mīr Dāmād drove went as far as the transmogrification of Ibn Sīnā himself into a neopythagorean occultist (World as (Arabic) Text, résumé; Powers of One, 1).

Ibn Sīnā heads the [[peripatetic-philosophy|anti-occultist coalition]] — with [[al-ghazali|Ghazālī]], Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Khaldūn — whose four centuries of polemic the [[pythagorean-cosmology|mathematicalization]] was built to answer. Two centuries after Ibn Turka, the tradition's chief prosecutor had been posthumously enrolled in the defence. The reclassification did not merely survive its opponents; it absorbed the most formidable of them.

What he settles about Ibn Turka

That the project continued. Melvin-Koushki calls this imperial neopythagorean lettrist crucial for understanding the continuity of the tradition. Ibn Turka died in an antechamber at Herat with half his work unedited, and it would be easy to read that as the end of the line. Mīr Dāmād is the evidence that it was not — the science reached the intellectual establishment of the next great Persian empire and helped define its philosophy.

And that the win was posthumous. Which is the shape of nearly everything in this biography: the [[samarkand-observatory|Samarkand tables]] carrying a lettrist preface under Shāhjahān, the [[bistami|Ottoman branch]] prospering while the Iranian one was tried, and now a Safavid philosopher completing the argument its author was exiled for.

What we do not know

  • Which three works the lettrist material appears in is not specified in the material in hand; Mīr Dāmād's on Doffing is the study to pursue.
  • Whether he read Ibn Turka directly. The inheritance is identified as Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu lettrism, not as a citation of the Mafāḥiṣ.
  • His birth date is not given in these sources.
  • The claim that Safavid occult science has been completely ignored is Melvin-Koushki's assessment of a field, and other specialists may weigh it differently.

What "largely responsible" is claiming

It is a causal claim about a whole philosophical culture, and it is worth separating into parts.

Safavid philosophy took a neopythagorean turn. That turn was effected substantially by one man's adoption of a specific inherited tradition — Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu philosophical [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] — in at least three of his works. And the mode in which that philosophy was actually lived, as a Safavid imperial way of life, was occult science (World as (Arabic) Text).

So the claim is not that Mīr Dāmād was influenced by lettrism. It is that lettrism is the mechanism by which Safavid philosophy became what it became, and he is the point of entry.

What that makes of Ibn Turka

The [[mulla-sadra|conventional]] account has Ibn Turka as a way-station on a road running from [[ibn-arabi|Ibn ʿArabī]] to Mullā Ṣadrā, valued for a synthesis of [[peripatetic-philosophy|peripatetic]], illuminationist and Akbarian positions on absolute being. On that account his lettrism is a private eccentricity that the main line routed around.

Melvin-Koushki's account inverts the topology. The lettrism is the main line. What travels from Timurid Iran into Safavid philosophy is the occult science, carried by a named figure in named works, and the metaphysics of wujūd is what the tradition talked about while doing it.

The reversal, once more

The turn went as far as the transmogrification of Ibn Sīnā himself into a neopythagorean occultist (World as (Arabic) Text; Powers of One, 1). Ibn Sīnā heads the coalition — with [[al-ghazali|Ghazālī]], Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Khaldūn — whose four centuries of polemic the whole [[pythagorean-cosmology|mathematicalization]] was built to answer.

Two centuries after Ibn Turka died waiting on a case review, the tradition's chief prosecutor had been enrolled in the defence. That is not merely survival; the reclassification absorbed the most formidable opponent it had.

The charge against the field

Recorded as he puts it, because it is a charge and not an emphasis: the Neoplatonic and perennialist-antiquarian character of Safavid philosophy is widely recognised, but few have admitted its equally remarkable neopythagorean turn — and occult science as the mode in which that philosophy was lived has been completely ignored, which makes a history of its practice impossible.

A field that has omitted the practice cannot write the history. That is the gap this portal sits in.
