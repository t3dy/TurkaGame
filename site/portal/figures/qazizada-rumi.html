Qazizada Rumi

Qāżīzāda Rūmī (c. 1380–1432), celebrated mathematician and astronomer. Member of the Isfahan Circle; friend and colleague to [[ibn-turka]]. Active in Samarkand at Timur's court and later in Isfahan and Yazd. Pioneer of mathematical methods in astronomy; integrated mathematical and occult approaches to celestial phenomena. Represents the mathematicization of Islamic astronomy during the early Timurid period.

Qāżīzāda Rūmī (also known as Ṣāliḥ al-Dīn Mūsā Qāḍīzāda, c. 1380–1432) was one of the most accomplished mathematicians and astronomers of the Timurid age. Born in Anatolia, he rose to prominence at Timur's court in Samarkand and later became a close colleague of [[ibn-turka]] in Isfahan and Yazd. Qazizada was renowned for advancing mathematical methods in astronomy—bringing rigorous calculation to celestial observations. He shared with Ibn Turka a commitment to integrating rational mathematical analysis with occult philosophy. Where Ibn Turka pursued lettrism as the universal science, Qazizada pursued mathematics and astronomy as paths to understanding cosmic principles. The two represent complementary approaches within the Isfahan Circle: Ibn Turka's linguistic-lettrist method and Qazizada's mathematical-astronomical method, both grounded in the belief that creation is knowable through precise systematic inquiry into its underlying patterns.
