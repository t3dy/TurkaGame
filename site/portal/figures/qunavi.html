Qūnavī

Ṣadr al-Dīn al-Qūnavī (1210–1274), leading disciple and systematizer of [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi's]] metaphysics. Through his philosophical elaboration of Ibn Arabi's doctrines, Qūnavī created the scholastic framework that transmitted Ibn Arabi's thought to later Islamic philosophers and occultists. His work provided the philosophical grounding for [[ibn-turka|Ibn Turka's]] integration of divine-names doctrine with lettrist and Pythagorean theory. A central figure in the Qūnavī-[[jandi|Jandī]] school tradition.

Ṣadr al-Dīn Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq al-Qūnavī (1210–1274) was the most important early systematizer of [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi's]] metaphysical doctrines. Born in Konya, Anatolia, Qūnavī became Ibn Arabi's foremost disciple and literary executor, tasked with organizing, elaborating, and defending his master's vision. Whereas Ibn Arabi's own writings are voluminous, imaginative, and often enigmatic, Qūnavī's works present Ibn Arabi's metaphysics in a rigorous, scholastic form, amenable to philosophical analysis and teaching. This systematization proved decisive: it was Qūnavī's articulation of Ibn Arabi's thought—not Ibn Arabi's own texts directly—that shaped the transmission of Ibn Arabi's legacy to later philosophers, Sufis, and occultists. Qūnavī's central contributions include: (1) the codification of the doctrine of the [[divine-names|divine names]] as the mechanism of creation; (2) the elaboration of the imaginal realm (malakūt) as the intermediate domain where mystical knowledge operates; (3) the establishment of the Perfect Human (al-insān al-kāmil) as the microcosm in which all divine attributes are manifest. Through the Qūnavī-[[jandi|Jandī]] school tradition, these doctrines reached Ottoman and Persian philosophers, providing the theoretical foundations that [[ibn-turka|Ibn Turka]] would deploy in systematizing [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrist occultism]] as the operative application of divine-names metaphysics.
