Isfahan Circle

Isfahan Circle (Dayira-yi Isfahan). The intellectual network centered on [[ibn-turka|Ibn Turka]] in late 14th- and early 15th-century Isfahan and Yazd. Members included [[sharaf-al-din-yazdi|Sharaf al-Din Yazdi]] (historian), [[qazizada-rumi|Qazizada Rumi]] (mathematician/astronomer), and other Timurid court scholars. Nexus of [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrist]] philosophy, [[pythagorean-cosmology|Pythagorean mathematics]], and political-administrative theory. The group represented a self-conscious intellectual project to synthesize Islamic occultism with reason, integrating mysticism, philosophy, and operative science.

New Brethren of Purity

Ikhwan al-Safa al-Jadid (The New Brethren of Purity). Transregional network of late 14th- and 15th-century scholars, occultists, and courtiers claiming succession to the 10th-century Brethren of Purity. Members included [[ibn-turka|Ibn Turka]], [[sayyid-husayn-akhlati|Akhlati]], [[sharaf-al-din-yazdi|Sharaf al-Din Yazdi]], and figures across Ottoman, Mamluk, and Persian courts. United by commitment to [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]], [[abjad-numerology|numerology]], and the integration of occult sciences with [[pythagorean-cosmology|Pythagorean philosophy]]. Represented a self-conscious intellectual movement to establish occultism as legitimate, rational universal science.

The Samarkand Observatory

The Samarkand Observatory, founded by [[ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] in 1420 around a meridian arc of unprecedented scale. Its Zīj-i Sulṭānī was the most accurate star table in the world for two centuries. Melvin-Koushki's argument places it inside Ibn Turka's story rather than beside it: the mathematization of astronomy achieved here and the [[neoplatonism|mathematicalization of the occult sciences]] are the same movement, and have been studied in isolation only by accident of modern disciplinary boundaries.
