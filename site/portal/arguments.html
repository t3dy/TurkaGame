Arguments

The historiographical claims this portal is organised around, stated as claims rather than left implicit in the entries. Each gives what is being argued, what it is argued against, the evidence, and why it matters. Some are Melvin-Koushki's; one is Ibn Turka's own. Those marked contested are positions actively being argued, not settled consensus.

Against the decline thesis

PERIODIZATION contested

Against the decline thesis. Post-classical Islamic thought is not a falling-off but a period of genuine innovation, and the fifteenth-century mathematicalization of the occult sciences is the case in point. The label Neopythagorean is chosen to make the comparison with the European Renaissance available without asserting contact.

The claim

Islamic intellectual history after the classical period is not a decline into scholasticism and mysticism but a record of continuous innovation, of which the fifteenth-century occult-scientific synthesis is a leading instance. Naming that achievement Neopythagorean places it in comparative frame: as the European Renaissance rediscovered and redeployed Platonic philosophy, Islamicate thinkers rediscovered and redeployed Pythagorean principles.

Against

The Orientalist periodisation in which Islamic thought peaks with Ibn Sīnā and Ghazālī and thereafter contracts — a frame that makes post-classical material uninteresting by definition and therefore unread.

Evidence

Ibn Turka's synthesis is demonstrably new rather than preservative: nobody had argued the occult sciences into the mathematical disciplines before. The Samarkand observatory and the Mafāḥiṣ begin in the same year. The tradition continued into Safavid philosophy through Mīr Dāmād, and into Mughal astronomy through the lettrist preface to the Zīj-i Shāhjahānī.

Why it matters

If the decline thesis holds, half of Ibn Turka's oeuvre can stay unedited without loss. If it does not, a major philosophical programme has been sitting unread in manuscript for six centuries because of an assumption about the period it belongs to.

The decline narrative remains the default framing in much survey literature; this is a revisionist position actively being argued rather than a settled consensus.

The occult sciences were moved into the mathematical sciences

ISLAMICATE_OCCULT

Mathematicalization and sanctification. The occult sciences were moved out of the natural sciences and into the mathematical ones, and simultaneously recast as sciences of walāya. Two moves for two audiences: mathematics answers the philosophers, sanctity answers the court.

The claim

Astrology, lettrism and geomancy were gradually but definitively shifted from the natural to the mathematical sciences as a means of reasserting their scientific legitimacy, and were simultaneously reclassified as the sciences of walāya — the second move being what explains the surge in court patronage before the Islamic millennium of 1592.

Against

Four centuries of anti-occultist polemic holding that these disciplines had no demonstrable subject — a coalition including Ibn Sīnā, Ghazālī, Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn Khaldūn, al-Ashʿarī, Fārābī, al-Bāqillānī and Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār.

Evidence

The reclassification is visible in how the sciences are taught, defended and positioned in the taxonomies of the period; in geomancy's overtly combinatorial procedure; and in the patronage record at Safavid, Mughal and Ottoman courts.

Why it matters

It supplies the mechanism the decline thesis lacks. And it explains why the answer had to be jurisdictional: you cannot out-cite Ghazālī, but mathematics is the one field where a question can be settled without appeal to authority.

Lettrism is the only truly universal science

IBN_TURKA contested

Ibn Turka's own claim, and the one everything else follows from. Not that lettrism deserves a place among the sciences, but that it supersedes philosophy on philosophy's own ground, because only the letter is the coincidentia oppositorum.

The claim

The faux-universal concepts of philosophical speculation notwithstanding, only the letter encompasses all that is and is not, all that can and cannot be; it alone is the coincidentia oppositorum; hence lettrism is the only truly universal science.

Against

Peripatetic philosophy's claim to treat being qua being, and so to be the most general science available.

Evidence

A philosophical concept ranges over the actual and the possible. The letter reaches also to non-being and impossibility, and so has the range a science of the whole requires. The Mafāḥiṣ supplies the technical apparatus: the letter analysed as counted, written and spoken, and then as it is in itself.

Why it matters

A maximal claim about the order of the sciences, made against the discipline that held the prestige — which is why it could not be quietly tolerated, and why the Tamhīd al-Qawāʿid shows he could have had a quiet career had he wanted one.

This is Ibn Turka's own claim, not a modern scholarly one. It was not conceded in his lifetime and cost him three tribunals.

Ḥurūfism does not define Islamicate lettrism

ISLAMICATE_OCCULT contested

Three lettrisms, not one. Gnostic-messianic, Sufi and intellectual. The Ḥurūfī movement is an instance of the first, not a definition of the field — and the difference is motive rather than method.

The claim

Lettrism runs in three strands — gnostic-messianic, Sufi, and intellectual — and Ibn Turka's unprecedented elevation of it against philosophy constitutes the third as a distinct thing. It is typically and erroneously assumed that the Ḥurūfī movement defines later Islamicate lettrism; what its vigour actually demonstrates is the religio-cultural valency of lettrism in Iran at all levels.

Against

The reflex that reads any fifteenth-century interest in letters as Ḥurūfī, and so as messianic and heterodox by association.

Evidence

Ibn Turka's millenarianism insists on the binding finality of the dispensation given through Muḥammad — a golden age of knowledge, not a new prophecy. The three movements are separable by motive: the Ḥurūfīs proclaim a new dispensation, the messianic Sufis promote themselves as saviours, the lettrists work within the tradition to expand human knowledge.

Why it matters

The distinction is what Ibn Turka's defence depended on, and collapsing it is what his prosecutors did in 1427. It is also what modern readers do when they file him with the Ḥurūfīs.

Explicitly framed as correcting a standing assumption in the field.

His fame is what buried him

IBN_TURKA

The acclaim caused the neglect. Being celebrated as the link between Ibn ʿArabī and Mullā Ṣadrā is what marginalised Ibn Turka's actual project — because the book that made that reputation contains no trace of it.

The claim

Ibn Turka's acclaim since the nineteenth century as the synthesizer linking Ibn ʿArabī to Mullā Ṣadrā is precisely what led to his marginalization: it obscures the occultist tenor of the larger project, abstracts it from its historical context, and robs it of its animating virtue.

Against

The seminary and scholarly consensus that presents him as a mystical philosopher lodged between Ṭūsī and Mullā Ṣadrā — Corbin's Shiʿi esotericist, Lewisohn's orthodox Sunni Sufi.

Evidence

The Tamhīd al-Qawāʿid is bare of any reference to his more central lettrist concerns, and it is the book on which his modern reputation rests almost exclusively. Over half the oeuvre — the lettrist works above all — remains unedited on the assumption that it is marginal. The apologies, the other main source for the received picture, were produced under great duress.

Why it matters

A working demonstration of how canon formation makes evidence invisible: the neglect looks like a judgement that specialists had assessed and set the material aside, when in fact they had never looked.

Ibn Khaldūn was not the sceptic he is taken for

COMPARATIVE contested

The exemplary sceptic defended magic. Ibn Khaldūn rejected astrology and alchemy but argued at length for the reality of magic and divination; Yazdī reached comparable conclusions about history from openly occultist premises. Neither man is where his reputation puts him.

The claim

Ibn Khaldūn's purported objectivity about the occult sciences is selective. He rejects astrology and alchemy out of hand, but argues forcefully, and at length, for the incontrovertible reality of magic and divination — with the selfsame sober objectivity. Meanwhile Yazdī's theories of historical development are not dissimilar to Ibn Khaldūn's while resting on a thoroughgoingly occultist outlook.

Against

The picture of Ibn Khaldūn as a shining, solitary example of rationality amidst rampant superstition — a man ahead of his time.

Evidence

His own text, read past the famous rejections. And on the other side, the structure of Yazdī's defence of geomancy: intention and mentation as mechanism, a failure condition located in the operator, and proofs by numerological correspondence to the divine name Light and to the name Daniel.

Why it matters

It removes the rationalism-versus-superstition frame from the period entirely. The argument between these two men is between two theories of history that disagree about which occult sciences are real — a harder and more interesting disagreement.

Runs against Ibn Khaldūn's standing as the exemplary rationalist of premodern Islamic thought.

The same science prospered in one polity and was prosecuted in another

COMPARATIVE

Same teacher, same science, opposite outcomes. Bisṭāmī's jafr manual became Ottoman state equipment; Ibn Turka was tried three times and died waiting on a case review. The variable is the polity, not the doctrine.

The claim

Ibn Turka and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān Bisṭāmī were both the intellectual heirs of Sayyid Ḥusayn Akhlāṭī, pursuing cognate universalist lettrist projects at opposite ends of one network. The Ottoman version was more successful, and the difference is political rather than doctrinal.

Against

Accounts that explain Ibn Turka's fate by the content of his ideas — as though a science of that kind must inevitably provoke a tribunal.

Evidence

Bisṭāmī's Miftāḥ al-jafr al-jāmiʿ came to function in the sixteenth century as a dynastic handbook, by which the political fate of the House of ʿOs̱mān could be managed over the generations. Ibn Turka faced three tribunals and an exile and died with half his work unedited. Shāhrukh had refounded Timurid legitimacy on the sharīʿa in 813/1411 and elevated the class of men who interpret it.

Why it matters

The strongest available argument that Ibn Turka's fate was contingent rather than deserved — and a warning against reading a thinker's reception as a verdict on his thought.

A history of Islamicate occult practice is currently impossible

METHOD contested

The practice has gone unstudied. Safavid philosophy's neopythagorean turn is unacknowledged and its occult practice ignored, which makes a history of that practice impossible to write. A charge against a field, recorded as such.

The claim

The Neoplatonic and perennialist-antiquarian character of Safavid philosophy is widely recognised, but few have admitted its equally remarkable neopythagorean turn — and the principal mode in which that philosophy was lived as an imperial way of life, namely occult science, has been completely ignored, which makes a history of its practice impossible.

Against

A historiography that studies the philosophy and omits what its practitioners actually did with it.

Evidence

Mīr Dāmād, held largely responsible for the neopythagoreanization of Safavid philosophy through adherence to Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu lettrism in at least three works, was known until recently simply as an occult scientist — the material was available and unread.

Why it matters

It is the gap this portal sits in, and the reason a reference work organised around one occult philosopher is worth building at all.

A charge against the state of a field, and other specialists may weigh it differently.
