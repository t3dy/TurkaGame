Sharḥ-i Naẓm al-Durr

Sharḥ-i Naẓm al-Durr — Ibn Turka's first major work, completed in Cairo in 806/1404 at the end of his fifteen years abroad. Formally a commentary on Ibn al-Fāriḍ's al-Tāʾiyya al-kubrā, it timuridizes the Mamluk anthology-as-commentary genre and is the first such commentary to re-poeticize the Ibn ʿArabī school tradition.

The work. Completed toward the end of Ibn Turka's Cairo residence, the Sharḥ-i Naẓm al-Durr is an ornate Persian commentary on Ibn al-Fāriḍ's (d. 1235) al-Tāʾiyya al-kubrā, a favourite teaching text of the [[ibn-arabi|Ibn ʿArabī]] school.

What is original in it. Melvin-Koushki shows that it closely tracks high Mamluk Arabic literary culture and effectively timuridizes the new hybrid Mamluk anthology-as-commentary genre, pioneered by Ibn Nubāta (d. 1366) and his student al-Ṣafadī (d. 1363) — both of whom also wrote seminal sword-versus-pen debates. It is the first such commentary to effectively re-poeticize that tradition, bringing each line of the poem into conjunction with several Persian or Arabic lines of verse, both contemporary and classical (Imperial Talismanic Love, 70).

Why it belongs in this portal. It is evidence about formation rather than doctrine. The man who would later argue that letters are cosmic principles began by writing a book about how verses answer to other verses — and he learned the form in Mamluk Cairo and carried it east. The lettrist project has a literary genealogy as well as a philosophical one.

It is also the base text for Akram Jūdī-Niʿmatī's edition, whose introduction supplies the list of works from which Melvin-Koushki's own annotated catalogue is built (The Quest, §2.1).
