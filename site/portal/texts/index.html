Texts

Book of Light

Kitab al-Nur (Book of Light). Ibn Turka's treatise on cosmological doctrines grounded in lettrist and Pythagorean principles. Expounds how divine light manifests through cosmic levels (intelligible, imaginal, sensible), how letters express this cascading illumination, and how human consciousness participates in cosmic light through lettrist gnosis.

Kitab al-Mafahis

Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ (Book of Inquiries). Ibn Turka's magnum opus and, in Melvin-Koushki's description, the first Arabic summa of Islamic neopythagoreanism. Begun shortly after Of Letters and almost certainly at the instance of his patron Iskandar Sulṭān; completed 823/1420, revised and expanded 828/1425. It sets out the fourfold analysis of the letter that is the technical core of his [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrist metaphysics]].

MS Majlis 10196

MS Majlis 10196 — the earliest, most complete and most reliable collection of Ibn Turka's works, copied over the last eight years of his life and three years past his death. The incipit and explicit of the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ]], ff. 52a and 118b, are in the author's own hand. It is the manuscript on which the dating of the whole oeuvre rests.

Munshaʾāt-i Ṣāʾin-i Turka

Munshaʾāt-i Ṣāʾin-i Turka — some fifty-eight letters, fragments and notes. The single richest biographical source for Ibn Turka, and the reason we know about his dealings with Timurid amirs, his closeness to [[baysunghur|Bāysunghur]], and his friendly relations with Sufis he criticised in print.

Nafsat al-Maṣdūr (the Apologies)

Nafsat al-Maṣdūr — Ibn Turka's apologies, written in self-defence during the tribunals and the exile. The second was composed at Herat during the nine months he spent in weekly attendance at court waiting for his case to be reviewed. They are the most personal thing he wrote and, for exactly that reason, the least reliable guide to what he actually thought.

Sharḥ-i Naẓm al-Durr

Sharḥ-i Naẓm al-Durr — Ibn Turka's first major work, completed in Cairo in 806/1404 at the end of his fifteen years abroad. Formally a commentary on Ibn al-Fāriḍ's al-Tāʾiyya al-kubrā, it timuridizes the Mamluk anthology-as-commentary genre and is the first such commentary to re-poeticize the Ibn ʿArabī school tradition.

Tamhīd al-Qawāʿid

Tamhīd al-Qawāʿid — a synthesis of Ibn Sīnā, Suhrawardī and Ibn ʿArabī/Qūnavī on absolute being, and by far the best known of Ibn Turka's works in the modern period. It is also, in the estimation of the author and his contemporaries, strictly of secondary importance. Its fame is the single largest reason his actual project was misread for a century.

Treatise on the Barzakh

Risalat al-Barzakh. Ibn Turka's dedicated treatise on the intermediate realm (barzakh) where causation operates. Expounds the metaphysical framework grounding all lettrist operations: how divine names become manifest in forms, how letters function as operative principles, how human will participates in cosmic causation through knowledge of the barzakh.

Treatise on the Divine Names

Ibn Turka's exposition of divine names and their correspondence to 28 letters. Demonstrates integration of Islamic theology with systematic magic.
