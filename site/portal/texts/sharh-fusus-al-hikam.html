Sharḥ Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam

Sharḥ Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam — Ibn Turka's commentary on [[ibn-arabi|Ibn ʿArabī's]] Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam, the central text of the Akbarian school. Completed 813/1411, with a corrected version in 817/1415. Evidence that he was a working Akbarian commentator and not merely an inheritor of Akbarian ideas — and part of why the modern reception filed him as a mystical philosopher.

Composition. Completed in 813/1411, the year he travelled to the Ḥijāz — possibly on pilgrimage, the chronology marks it with a question mark — and invited [[sharaf-al-din-yazdi|Sharaf al-Dīn]] to join him. A corrected version followed in 817/1415 (The Quest, chronology).

The base text. The Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam is [[ibn-arabi|Ibn ʿArabī's]] most studied work and the standard entry point to the Akbarian tradition. Commenting on it is how a scholar joins that conversation, and the commentary tradition around it — [[qunavi|Qūnavī]], [[jandi|Jandī]] and their successors — is the school Ibn Turka inherited.

What it establishes. That the Akbarian material in the [[tamhid-al-qawaid|Tamhīd al-Qawāʿid]] is not a veneer. He did the philological and exegetical work; he was competent in the school's central text at the level required to comment on it. When the portal argues that his reputation as an Akbarian synthesist obscured his occultism, it is not arguing that the reputation was unearned.

The revision, and a caution about dates. He returned to it four years later. He did the same with the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Mafāḥiṣ]], expanded in 828/1425, five years after completion. Ibn Turka revisited his own texts, which matters when reading colophons: a date in [[ms-majlis-10196|MS Majlis 10196]] may record a second pass rather than a first composition, on top of the general difficulty that composition and transcription often cannot be distinguished.

What we do not know. What the 817/1415 corrections changed; whether the Ḥijāz journey was a pilgrimage; whether Yazdī accepted the invitation; and whether the commentary contains lettrist material.

Why commenting on the Fuṣūṣ is a credential

The Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam is the most studied of [[ibn-arabi|Ibn ʿArabī's]] works and the standard entry point to the Akbarian tradition. A commentary on it is how a scholar demonstrates membership — the text is difficult, the existing commentary tradition ([[qunavi|Qūnavī]], [[jandi|Jandī]] and their successors) is dense, and a commentator has to show command of both.

So this work settles something the portal needs settled: the Akbarian material in the [[tamhid-al-qawaid|Tamhīd]] is not a veneer. Ibn Turka did the philological work. When the portal argues that his reputation as an Akbarian synthesist obscured his occultism, it is not arguing that the reputation was unearned — which would be a much weaker and less interesting claim.

The revision, and a caution it forces

He completed it in 813/1411 and produced a corrected version in 817/1415 — four years later, and one year after [[iskandar-falls|Iskandar's destruction]]. He did the same with the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Mafāḥiṣ]], expanded in 828/1425, five years after completion.

Ibn Turka revisited his own texts, and that habit has a methodological consequence. The chronology of the oeuvre rests on the colophons of [[ms-majlis-10196|MS Majlis 10196]], where in many instances it is already impossible to tell whether a date records composition or transcription. Add an author who revises, and a third possibility appears: the date may record a second pass over an older work. Any single date in this corpus is carrying more uncertainty than it looks.

The year it belongs to

813/1411 is a dense year. He travelled to the Ḥijāz — possibly on pilgrimage, the chronology marks it with a question mark — and invited [[sharaf-al-din-yazdi|Sharaf al-Dīn]] to join him. In the same year [[shahrukh|Shāhrukh]] abrogated the yasa, enforced the sharīʿa and closed the taverns, refounding Timurid legitimacy on religious grounds.

What we do not know

What the corrections changed; whether the journey was a pilgrimage; whether Yazdī accepted; and whether the commentary contains lettrist material — which would date his lettrism earlier than the [[risalat-huruf|817/1414]] treatise does.
