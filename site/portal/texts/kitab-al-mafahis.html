Kitāb al-Mafāḥīs: The Foundation of Ibn Turka's System

The Kitāb al-Mafāḥīs fī Asrār al-Ḥurūf wa-l-Awfāq (Book of Inquiries into the Secrets of Letters and Talismanic Squares) is Ibn Turka's most comprehensive statement. Written early 15th century (c. 1400–1420 CE / 804–824 AH), it consolidated decades of innovation into systematic treatise transforming Islamic lettrism from scattered gnosis into rigorous science.

Structure and Scope

The Kitāb al-Mafāḥīs organizes around three themes:

1. The Science of Letters ('Ilm al-Ḥurūf) — ontological and operative theory of 28 Arabic letters, each corresponding to [[divine-names|divine name]] and cosmic principle

2. Talismanic Squares (al-Awfāq) — practical application of letter and number combinations to create talismans and operative devices

3. Pythagorean Cosmology — underlying [[pythagorean-cosmology|mathematical structure]] uniting letters, numbers, names, and principles

Key Doctrines

Ibn Turka establishes:

  • Barzakh as operative domain: [[barzakh|Intermediate realm]] is actual location where [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrist operations]] produce material effects
  • Abjad as cosmic mathematics: [[abjad-numerology|Letter-value systems]] reflect fundamental cosmic ratios. Composing words with specific numerical sums aligns practitioners with cosmic forces.
  • Letters as divine attributes: Each letter expresses one [[divine-names|divine name]]. Knowledge of letters is knowledge of God's attributes.
  • Universality: Unlike localized traditions, this system applies to all creation—creation itself constituted through letters and numbers.

Transmission and Influence

The Kitāb al-Mafāḥīs circulated widely and influenced later occultists, philosophers, and European Renaissance Hermetic synthesis. In the Islamicate world it became canonical—cited by Ottoman occultists, incorporated into Safavid philosophy, foundational to "New Brethren of Purity" doctrines.

Historiographical Significance

The work proves Islamic occultism in 15th century was living, systematic, intellectually rigorous discipline—not decaying remnant. Demonstrates Ibn Turka engaged in genuine philosophical innovation: radical synthesis of lettrism with Pythagorean mathematics. Underwrites Melvin-Koushki's argument against decline thesis.
