Risāla al-Bāʾiyya

Risāla al-Bāʾiyya — a treatise on a single letter, and on a real puzzle. B is not among the light letters but the first of the dark ones and fountainhead of all duality. So why does it open both the Qur'an and the Torah, when A — avatar of divine Oneness and of the beginning of creation — is so much better suited to the purpose? One of four treatises from 828/1425.

The puzzle

The Bāʾiyya addresses what Melvin-Koushki calls the central cosmogonic conundrum of Ibn Turka's system, and it can be stated in three sentences.

The Arabic letters divide into light (nūrānī) and dark (ẓulmānī). The light ones are the fourteen isolated letters (muqaṭṭaʿāt) that open twenty-nine Sūras — exactly half the alphabet, and naturally a primary matrix of lettrism. B is not among them. It is the first of the dark letters and the fountainhead of all duality.

And yet B opens both the Qur'an and the Torah — those mathematical-linguistic models of the cosmos. Surely the primordial singularity of A, as avatar both of divine Oneness and of the beginning of creation, is far better suited to a monotheistic and monotheurgic purpose (Being with a Capital B, 2).

Why this is a serious problem and not a curiosity

Because the whole system stands or falls on the letters being non-arbitrary. If the values and ranks of the letters are conventional, [[abjad-numerology|abjad]] is bookkeeping and [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] is a game. Ibn Turka's claim is that they are facts about the order of being.

Given that claim, scripture opening on the fountainhead of duality rather than on the avatar of Oneness is not an oddity to be noted. It is a datum that appears to falsify the theory — the two most authoritative texts in existence beginning at the wrong place. A system that could not answer this would have a hole in it exactly where its authority comes from.

How he answers

He deftly combines rational and traditional arguments to riddle it (Being with a Capital B, 2) — which is the method in miniature: not scriptural citation alone, not philosophical demonstration alone, but the two run together, which is what the claim to be a [[occult-science-universal|universal science]] requires.

The larger article takes its title from this treatise: Being with a Capital B, on Ibn Turka's imaginal ontology. Duality is not a defect in creation to be explained away but the condition of there being a creation at all — and a scripture that begins there begins where existence does.

Where it sits

One of four treatises the chronology assigns to 828/1425, alongside the R. al-Inzāliyya, the R. Anjām and the R. Nuqṭa — the same year the [[kitab-al-mafahis|Mafāḥiṣ]] was revised and expanded, and inside the window in which two-thirds of the surviving oeuvre was copied into [[ms-majlis-10196|MS Majlis 10196]]. Two years before the exile.

A treatise named for a single letter, sitting beside one named for the point (nuqṭa), suggests a project of short focused studies on the elements of the system, undertaken while its author's position was collapsing.

What we do not know

  • His actual solution. The article states the conundrum and the method; the argument itself is in the treatise, which is not edited in the corpus in hand.
  • Whether the four 828/1425 treatises form a set with a common plan.
  • Whether the date records composition or copying — the general caution about MS Majlis 10196 applies.
