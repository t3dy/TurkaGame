Kitāb al-Manāhij

Kitāb al-Manāhij — a manual of logic, written in exile while wintering at Natanz in 833/1430, for his son Jamāl al-Dīn Muḥammad. The most domestic thing in the oeuvre: a man three years into political disgrace, whose career was being dismantled on charges of doctrinal irregularity, writing his son the elementary textbook.

Composition. In 833/1430 Ibn Turka wintered at Natanz and sent for his family; while there he wrote the Kitāb al-Manāhij for his son Jamāl al-Dīn Muḥammad (The Quest, chronology and 41). It falls between the failed attempt to obtain a hearing at Simnan in 833/1429 and the promise of reinstatement at Ṣāʾin Qalʿa around 834/1430.

What it is. A manual of logic — manāhij meaning methods or paths. Not a lettrist work, not an apology, not a commission for a prince. Logic is the propaedeutic, the discipline taught first because everything else is conducted in it.

Why the choice is worth noticing. Ibn Turka had spent three decades arguing that [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] supersedes philosophy, and was at that moment paying for it. What he wrote for his own son was the philosophers' entry-level textbook. Read one way that is a father giving a boy the credential that keeps him safe; read another it is a man who never doubted that the rigorous disciplines were the ground his own claims had to be defended on. The [[peripatetic-philosophy|peripatetic]] entry argues he was a competent logician before he was a critic of logic's pretensions, and this is the evidence.

The family thread. Of ten children — three sons, seven daughters — only two sons are known by name, and both through texts. Jamāl al-Dīn Muḥammad receives this book; Najīb al-Dīn Ḥabīb Allāh appears to have written a brief lettrist treatise of his own, the Qavāʿid-i Jafr-i Jāmiʿ. The dissertation notes that Jamāl al-Dīn may have been a key player in something the passage in hand does not complete.

What we do not know. Whether it survives, and in what state; whether it was ever edited; whether Jamāl al-Dīn used it; and what the unfinished sentence about his role was going to say.
