Concepts

16 entries.

'Ilm al-ḥurūf

'Ilm al-ḥurūf (the science of letters). Islamic science investigating the divine and cosmological meanings hidden in the forms, sounds, and numerical values of Arabic letters. The doctrine that Creation is fundamentally linguistic—that God speaks the world into being through the Arabic alphabet—and that mastery of letter-properties (form, sound, numerical value, and correspondence to divine names and cosmic principles) confers access to hidden causal powers. Central to Ibn Turka's entire philosophical system and foundational to late medieval and early modern Islamic occultism.

Abjad numerology

Abjad numerology (Hisab al-Jumul). The system assigning numerical values to Arabic letters (alif=1, bāʾ=2, etc.), allowing letters and words to be manipulated mathematically. Central to [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]]: Ibn Turka used abjad values to establish correspondences between divine names, cosmic principles, and human faculties. Enables the calculation of ''operative words'' whose numerical sum aligns with specific celestial or talismanic purposes.

TECHNIQUE

Barzakh

Al-Barzakh (the Isthmus). In Ibn Turka's system, the intermediate realm between pure matter and pure spirit, between multiplicity and unity. The domain where letters become forms, where [[divine-names]] manifest as causal powers, where mathematics governs physical existence. Central to understanding how lettrism operates—letters do not merely represent reality but constitute the barzakh through which divine principles become concrete.

COSMOLOGY

Mamluk Cairo Context

Cairo was the intellectual and political center of the Mamluk Sultanate (1250–1517). Akhlati and other Ibn Turka's predecessors worked in Cairo courts. Mamluk patronage of scholars influenced Timurid court structures.

INSTITUTION_TERM

Neoplatonism

Neoplatonism. The late-antique emanationist philosophy of Plotinus and Proclus, which reached Islamdom in Arabic translation and was never, in Melvin-Koushki's usage, quite separable from its Pythagorean twin — he writes of neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy as one tradition. Ibn Turka's contribution was not to receive it but to reclassify it: he treated this philosophy as prophetically revealed rather than merely Greek, and made its mathematics the ground on which the occult sciences could be defended as sciences at all.

EPISTEMOLOGY

Occult Science as Universal System

Occult Science as Universal System. The core historiographical claim that Ibn Turka and the Isfahan Circle systematized Islamic lettrism, talismanism, and numerology into a comprehensive philosophy claiming universal validity—a science encompassing metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, cosmology, and operative practice. Not marginal superstition but legitimate intellectual achievement rivaling any philosophical school. Melvin-Koushki's central argument.

HISTORIOGRAPHIC

Peripatetic Philosophy

Al-Falsafa al-Mashaʾiya (Peripatetic Philosophy). The Aristotelian philosophical tradition as transmitted and developed in Islamic world. Emphasizes rational inquiry, logical syllogism, study of natural world, metaphysics grounded in being and causation. Ibn Turka synthesized peripatetic logic and metaphysics with [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] and [[pythagorean-cosmology|Pythagorean mathematics]], arguing that occult science achieves what peripatetic philosophy merely attempts—systematic knowledge of causation and being.

EPISTEMOLOGY

Pythagorean Cosmology

Pythagorean Cosmology. The ancient Greek doctrine that numbers and their ratios are the fundamental principles governing reality. Ibn Turka's distinctive contribution was grounding Islamic [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] explicitly in Pythagorean mathematics, arguing that letters and their [[abjad-numerology|numerical values]] encode cosmic principles just as Pythagoras taught that mathematics is the language of creation. This synthesis gave Islamic lettrism mathematical legitimacy and positioned it as a truly universal science encompassing reason, mysticism, and operative power.

COSMOLOGY

Shiʿi-Sunnism (imamophilism)

Shiʿi-Sunnism, or imamophilism — reverence for the Imams held without a formal Shiʿi confessional commitment. Melvin-Koushki calls it a defining feature of 9th/15th-century Islamic religiosity, particularly in Iran and particularly in esotericist circles, and it is his answer to the long argument about whether Ibn Turka was 'really' a Shiʿi or a Sunni. The question is anachronistic; the position is the period's own.

THEOLOGICAL

Sufism

Al-Tasawwuf (Sufism). Islamic mysticism emphasizing direct experiential knowledge of the divine through discipline, contemplation, and often ecstatic experience. Developed systematically from 9th century onward; by Ibn Turka's era, Sufism was integral to Islamic orthodoxy. [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]], [[suhrawardi|Suhrawardi]], [[al-ghazali|Al-Ghazali]], and other philosophical predecessors were Sufi-philosophers. Ibn Turka inherited and operationalized Sufi gnosis through lettrism, positioning occult science as Sufi practice systematized into rational discipline.

THEOLOGICAL

Talismanic Science

ilm al-Talasim (Talismanic Science). Operative practice of creating talismans inscribed with letters, numbers, divine names. Based on theory that letters, numerical correspondences, and divine names are cosmic principles that, combined correctly with proper intention, exert force through intermediate realm. Central practical application of Ibn Turka's system.

OCCULT_SCIENCE

The Divine Names

Al-Asmaʾ al-Ilāhīyya (The Divine Names and Attributes). Islamic doctrine positing 28 (or 99) divine names—each expressing an aspect of God's nature (The Merciful, The Just, The Mighty, etc.). In [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]], each of the 28 Arabic letters corresponds to one divine name, making letters themselves expressions of divine attributes. Ibn Turka's system treats mastery of the divine names through their corresponding letters as the path to aligning human will with cosmic principles.

THEOLOGICAL

The Imaginal Realm

Al-ʿAlam al-Khiyāl (The Imaginal Realm). In [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi's]] metaphysics, the ontological layer between pure intellect and material sense where forms (ashkāl) exist independently of physical matter. The domain where mystical visions occur, where symbols manifest, where divine names express themselves in subtle forms. Central to understanding how knowledge operates in Islamic mysticism and occultism: the imaginal realm is where letters and divine names acquire form, where talismanic operations work, where the barzakh functions.

COSMOLOGY

Timurid Court Patronage

The institutional and political context enabling Ibn Turka and Isfahan Circle. Timurid rulers patronized scholars, established courts as intellectual centers, created conditions for systematic philosophical innovation.

INSTITUTION_TERM

Walāya

Walāya — sainthood, or the authority that comes of proximity to God. The most consequential reclassification in Melvin-Koushki's account, and the one usually missed: alongside their move into the mathematical sciences, the occult sciences were recast as sciences of walāya. This, he argues, is what alone explains the surge in patronage of professional occultists at the Safavid, Mughal and Ottoman courts in the runup to the Islamic millennium (1592 CE). A science of walāya is not a technique anyone may practise; it is the prerogative of the sanctified.

THEOLOGICAL

Ḥurūfism

Ḥurūfism — the messianic letter-movement founded by Fażl Allāh Astarābādī (d. 796/1394), which read the letters as announcing a new dispensation. It is the heresy Ibn Turka had to be distinguished from, and the charge on which he was exiled from Herat in 1427. The distinction is not technique but motive: the Ḥurūfīs proclaimed a new revelation; the lettrists claimed to be working within the tradition to extend human knowledge.
