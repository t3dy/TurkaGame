Neoplatonism

Neoplatonism. The late-antique emanationist philosophy of Plotinus and Proclus, which reached Islamdom in Arabic translation and was never, in Melvin-Koushki's usage, quite separable from its Pythagorean twin — he writes of neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy as one tradition. Ibn Turka's contribution was not to receive it but to reclassify it: he treated this philosophy as prophetically revealed rather than merely Greek, and made its mathematics the ground on which the occult sciences could be defended as sciences at all.

Neoplatonism, and what Ibn Turka did to it

The tradition. Neoplatonism — the emanationist system of Plotinus (d. 270), systematised by Porphyry and Proclus — holds that reality flows from a transcendent One through descending grades of being, and that the soul ascends by retracing that descent. It entered Arabic through the translation movement, generally fused with Pythagorean number-metaphysics. Melvin-Koushki treats the pair as a single tradition throughout, writing of neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy rather than of two separable schools (Powers of One, 127–28).

The problem it was made to solve. By Ibn Turka's lifetime the occult sciences — al-ʿulūm al-gharība, the 'strange sciences' — had absorbed four centuries of hostile polemic from a formidable coalition. Melvin-Koushki notes that anti-occultism is one of the few subjects on which philosophers like Ibn Sīnā, theologians like Ghazālī, traditionalists like Ibn Taymiyya and historians like Ibn Khaldūn find common cause, joined by al-Ashʿarī, Fārābī, al-Bāqillānī and Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār (Powers of One, 1). The charge was that these were not sciences.

Ibn Turka's contribution. The Persianate answer, of which he is the central theoretician, was to move the occult sciences out of the natural sciences and into the mathematical sciences — a reclassification that reasserted their legitimacy on ground the polemicists could not easily contest (Powers of One, 1). Melvin-Koushki names the threefold process mathematicalization, neopythagoreanization and sanctification, and locates it in Ilkhanid–Timurid–Aqquyunlu Iran. Neoplatonism is the metaphysics that makes the move coherent: if being descends by graded emanation, and number governs the descent, then a science of number and letter is a science of causation itself.

The move that was genuinely his. Ibn Turka did not present this philosophy as a Greek inheritance. He identified its greatest exponents — the Imams and the ancients, with their disciples in every age — with the quranic phrase ūlū l-aydī wa-l-abṣār, 'those possessed of hands and vision': the coterie of inspired thinkers who preserved prophetically revealed neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy in written form. The philosophy is therefore not borrowed but recovered, and its authority revelatory rather than merely rational. The phrase appears to have entered common usage as a designation for sages and philosophers generally from Ibn Turka onward (Of Islamic Grammatology, 66).

What changed. The consequences ran well past his death. Melvin-Koushki argues that this mathematicalization is the immediate intellectual and sociopolitical context both for the celebrated mathematization of astronomy by the Samarkand Observatory in the 9th/15th century and for the resurgence of neoplatonic-neopythagorean philosophy in Safavid Iran in the 10th/16th and 11th/17th — in the course of which Ibn Sīnā himself, the tradition's most formidable anti-occultist, was posthumously transformed into a neopythagorean-occultist (Powers of One, 1). These processes, he notes, have previously been studied in atomistic isolation.

Why the pair is never separated

Melvin-Koushki writes throughout of neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy as one thing, and the hyphen is a considered position rather than shorthand. In late antiquity the two were already fused: Plotinus and Proclus are read through number, and the Pythagorean material reaches the Arabic tradition inside a Neoplatonic frame rather than beside it.

The consequence for reading this portal is that emanation and number are two descriptions of one structure. Emanation says being descends by grades; number says the grades stand in determinate ratios. A science that works the ratios is therefore working the descent, which is what licenses the claim that [[ilm-al-huruf|a science of letters]] is a science of causation.

The afterlife, and the reversal

The most striking evidence that this framework won is what happened to its opponent. Melvin-Koushki tracks a resurgence of neoplatonic-neopythagorean philosophy in Safavid Iran in the 10th/16th and 11th/17th centuries, in the course of which Ibn Sīnā himself was transformed into a neopythagorean-occultist (Powers of One, 1).

[[mir-damad|Mīr Dāmād]] is the figure held largely responsible, through adherence to a particular brand of Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu philosophical lettrism in at least three of his works. The chain in that phrase is the transmission history compressed: Cairo, then Ibn Turka's Iran, then the Turkman courts, then Safavid Isfahan.

The claim about the field

Worth recording as Melvin-Koushki puts it, because it is a charge and not just an emphasis. The Neoplatonic and perennialist-antiquarian character of Safavid philosophy is, he grants, widely recognised. But few have admitted its equally remarkable neopythagorean turn — and the principal mode in which that philosophy was lived as a Safavid imperial way of life, namely occult science, has been completely ignored, which makes a history of its practice impossible (World as (Arabic) Text).
