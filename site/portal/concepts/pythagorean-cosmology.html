Pythagorean Cosmology

Pythagorean Cosmology. The ancient Greek doctrine that numbers and their ratios are the fundamental principles governing reality. Ibn Turka's distinctive contribution was grounding Islamic [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] explicitly in Pythagorean mathematics, arguing that letters and their [[abjad-numerology|numerical values]] encode cosmic principles just as Pythagoras taught that mathematics is the language of creation. This synthesis gave Islamic lettrism mathematical legitimacy and positioned it as a truly universal science encompassing reason, mysticism, and operative power.

Pythagorean Cosmology in Islamic Context

Pythagorean philosophy (c. 580–500 BCE) teaches that numbers are the fundamental constituents of reality. Numbers, through their ratios and relationships, generate harmony, order, and all manifestation. This worldview emphasizes that the cosmos is knowable through mathematics, and that mathematical knowledge grants insight into (and potentially influence over) the structure of creation.

The late-antique Neoplatonic tradition preserved and developed Pythagorean thought, eventually reaching the Islamic world through Hellenistic texts, Byzantine commentaries, and the writings of Islamic philosophers like al-Kindī and al-Fārābī. Yet lettrism in the Islamic world developed largely independently, growing from Qur'anic exegesis and gnosis without explicit Pythagorean framing.

Ibn Turka's Innovation

Ibn Turka's genius was explicitly connecting Islamic lettrism with Pythagorean principles. He argued that:

1. Letters are numbers: Each of the 28 Arabic letters has a numerical value ([[abjad-numerology|abjad]]) 2. Numbers are cosmic: Pythagorean mathematics describes the structure of creation 3. Therefore, letters ARE cosmic principles: Mastery of letters is mastery of the mathematics of creation

This move was philosophically powerful: it gave lettrism a pedigree reaching back to ancient Greece; it grounded it in mathematical reason rather than mystical intuition alone; and it positioned it as the supreme science, encompassing both rational (Pythagorean math) and mystical (gnostic lettrism) approaches.

By adopting the Pythagorean framework, Ibn Turka essentially reinvented lettrism as a Neopythagorean system—and in doing so, positioned Islamic occult philosophy as a genuine alternative universal science, rivaling and arguably exceeding the philosophical systems of contemporary Renaissance Europe.

What the label is claiming

"Neopythagorean" is Melvin-Koushki's term of art and it is chosen against an alternative. The conventional description of post-classical Islamic thought is decline: the Greek inheritance received, digested, and then dissipated into scholasticism and mysticism. Calling the fifteenth-century development Neopythagorean places it instead in a comparative frame — as the European Renaissance rediscovered and redeployed Platonic philosophy, so Islamicate thinkers rediscovered and redeployed Pythagorean principles.

The claim is therefore double: that something genuinely new happened, and that its shape is recognisable from the European case without being derived from it.

The move it names

The concrete content is the reclassification described at [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] and [[walaya|walāya]]: the occult sciences were shifted from the natural to the mathematical sciences, reasserting their legitimacy against four centuries of polemic, and simultaneously recast as sciences of sanctity (Powers of One, 1).

Why mathematics specifically? Because the anti-occultist attack was that these disciplines had no demonstrable subject matter — that they dealt in sympathies and correspondences no one could check. Mathematics was the one field whose credentials nobody disputed. Moving [[ilm-al-huruf|lettrism]] into it is a jurisdictional claim before it is a metaphysical one.

Revealed, not borrowed

Ibn Turka's own framing is not that he is importing Greek philosophy. He identifies the greatest exponents of the perennial philosophy — the Imams and the ancients with their disciples in every age — with the Qur'anic ūlū l-aydī wa-l-abṣār, those possessed of hands and vision, who preserved prophetically revealed neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy in written form (Of Islamic Grammatology, 66).

The philosophy is thus recovered rather than adopted, and its authority is revelatory rather than merely rational. The phrase appears to have entered general usage for sages and philosophers from Ibn Turka onward — a small piece of evidence that the framing took.

The consequences downstream

Melvin-Koushki argues the same movement is the immediate context for the mathematization of astronomy at the [[samarkand-observatory|Samarkand Observatory]] and for the resurgence of neoplatonic-neopythagorean philosophy in Safavid Iran — in the course of which Ibn Sīnā himself, the tradition's most formidable anti-occultist, was posthumously transformed into a neopythagorean-occultist (Powers of One, 1). The reclassification eventually captured its own chief opponent.

What we do not know

  • Which Pythagorean sources were actually available to Ibn Turka in Arabic, and in what form, is not detailed in this corpus.
  • How much of the framing is his and how much Melvin-Koushki's analytical vocabulary requires the unedited works to settle.

Number as the ground of verification

The move into the mathematical sciences is usually described as a bid for prestige, and it was that. But there is a stronger reason, and it belongs to the [[tahqiq-taqlid|taḥqīq]] axis.

Mathematics is the discipline in which verification is possible without appeal to authority. A demonstration either goes through or it does not, and no master's endorsement improves it. For a science under four centuries of attack from a coalition that included the most authoritative names available — Ibn Sīnā, [[al-ghazali|Ghazālī]], Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn Khaldūn — that property is not merely respectable, it is the only ground on which the argument could be won at all. You cannot out-cite Ghazālī. You can, in principle, out-demonstrate him.

This is why [[geomancy|geomancy]] is one of the three sciences named in the reclassification alongside astrology and lettrism: the randomness is at the input only, and everything downstream is deterministic combination. What a geomancer does after the marks are made is calculate.

Revealed rather than borrowed, and why that framing was necessary

Ibn Turka does not present this as an importation of Greek philosophy. He identifies the perennial philosophers — the Imams and the ancients with their disciples in every age — with the Qur'anic ūlū l-aydī wa-l-abṣār, those possessed of hands and vision, who preserved prophetically revealed neopythagorean-neoplatonic philosophy in written form (Of Islamic Grammatology, 66).

The framing does two jobs at once. It gives the philosophy revelatory authority rather than merely rational authority, which matters in front of a tribunal. And it makes the science recovered rather than adopted, so that pursuing it is a form of fidelity rather than of innovation — a defence with real force in a tradition where bidʿa, innovation, is a charge.

That the phrase appears to have entered general usage for sages and philosophers from Ibn Turka onward is small evidence that the framing took.
