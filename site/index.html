TurkaGame

Games and research from the world of Ibn Turka, 1369–1432

Built from extant manuscripts and real scholarship on Ṣāʾin al-Dīn ʿAlī ibn Turka Iṣfahānī — Chief Judge of Isfahan and the foremost occult philosopher of Timurid Iran, whose Pythagorean lettrist cosmology shaped imperial culture across the Timurid, Aqquyunlu, Safavid, Uzbek, Ottoman, and Mughal courts. Every scene and article carries its grounding: what the sources attest, what merely fits them, and what is invented.

Ibn Turka: The Occult Court

career roguelike · current build

A whole life in five phases — Cairo 1385 to exile 1432. 70 grounded encounters, an obligation-and-patronage economy, compounding political exposure, and an ending that judges the man and his system on separate axes. Historical terms are glossed as you read them — and at the end you can publish the whole playthrough to a permanent URL, with a private link that lets a reader correct the text and write in the margins.

Yūsuf Ascent

puzzle minigame · three prototypes

One painting — Bihzād’s Yūsuf fleeing Zulaykha, Herat 1488 — cut into 43 interactable elements and put back together three ways: a seven-door hotspot chain, an exploded 3D stack that resolves back into the exact painting from one privileged viewpoint, and a drag-sort of the palace into its seven cosmological strata. A research portal underneath carries the whole argument.

The Visionary Gallery

22 folios · measured, cut up, playable

Twenty-two Persianate folios — ascension cycles, planetary pavilions, demons on bare paper, a book of omens, and six folios from the same manuscript as Bihzād’s impossible palace — fetched with their licences checked by script, measured, auto-decomposed, turned into printable paper tunnel books and 3D stacks, and made playable. It also measures how much worse the machine is than the hand-drawn version: it ranks regions much as the argument does and finds only 27% of them.

The project

Three prototypes were planned; two are playable. The roguelike remains at design-doc stage. Assets are real manuscript images with provenance records — institution, shelfmark, rights — never invented pastiche, and no invented portraits of Ibn Turka himself. Built on the research of Matthew Melvin-Koushki, quoted with permission.

Older builds and superseded versions live in the archive. Source and documentation: github.com/t3dy/TurkaGame.
