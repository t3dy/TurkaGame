## Core move

A counterfactual: John Dee's real bids for imperial patronage in England and the
Habsburg lands all failed, and he died poor with his library destroyed. What if he had
instead gone east, following English merchants, to seek patronage at the Ottoman court,
which was — per Melvin-Koushki — considerably more occult-friendly than its Christian
counterparts at the time?

## Useful period texture

- Sultan Murad III (r. 1574–95) is framed as a Sufi initiate devoted to oneiromancy,
  astrology, physiognomy, and talismanry — "sister science" to Jewish kabbalah — and as
  someone who fashioned himself a prophet-saint-king on the strength of personal
  spiritual/angelic communications, paralleling Dee's own angel-conversations.
  Reign spans the Islamic millennium (992 AH / 1592 CE).
- The *Book of Soyga* (garbled "Arabic book," Uriel/Michael lore in Dee's own angelic
  diaries) is argued to derive from the Bunian-Bistamian magical corpus, popular in
  Ottoman courtly/scholarly circles.
- Ibn Turka is directly named here as the "Islamic answer to [Dee's] Hieroglyphic
  Monad": the **Tahawi Circle** from *Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ* (cited as Tehran, MS Majlis
  10196, f. 63a) is presented as Ibn Turka's own sigil-summary of his life's work,
  functionally parallel to Dee's Monad.
- Explicit framing line: "Ibn Turka is best approached as a Timurid Dr. Dee — or Dee
  best approached as an Elizabethan Dr. Littleturk." Both committed to mathematical,
  magical, kabbalistic/lettrist, alchemical, and anagogic unification of knowledge in
  service of a millenarian, one-world empire; both smeared in their own day, both more
  influential after death.
- Contrast point: Ibn Turka's scientific program "paid off" immediately under three
  Timurid patrons — most notably Ulugh Beg (r. 1409–49), builder of the Samarkand
  Observatory, whose star tables were still in use in 19th-century London.
- Mughal Emperor Akbar (r. 1556–1605) is offered as the most successful *avatar* of this
  millennial occult-scientific sovereignty model, syncretizing Muslim, Hindu, Jain, and
  Christian traditions into "modern India."

## Design relevance

- Good source for a Dee-analog NPC or a comparative "what if" framing device in the
  visual novel (a Western occultist character crossing into the Islamicate world, mirroring
  Ibn Turka's own reception history).
- Good source for Ottoman-flavored court content if a later prototype wants to extend
  past the Timurid setting.
