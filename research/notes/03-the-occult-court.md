## Core move

Uses a minor Persian manual of illusionism and trickery, *Boon for the Khan* (by the
Herati Naqshbandī Sufi, preacher, and occultist ʿAlī Ṣafī, d. 1533 — son of Ḥusayn Vāʿiẓ
Kāshifī), as a window onto how occult science functioned as literal political technology
across the shared Mamluk-Timurid-Aqquyunlu-Safavid-Uzbek-Ottoman-Mughal court culture.
Includes a full translated excerpt of the text.

## Court culture / political economy (the load-bearing facts)

- Across this entire post-Mongol Persianate/Chinggisid-Islamic world, courts adopted a
  shared Timurid Pythagorean-Platonic astrological-lettrist platform as default imperial
  cosmology and political science. Talismanic books, bodies, and buildings proliferated
  at a scale not seen before.
- Ibn Turka and his colleagues are named directly as founders of this platform, via the
  "New Brethren of Purity" network — traced onward through Sharaf al-Dīn ʿAlī Yazdī
  (historian/mathematician, Samarkand Observatory) as co-architect of the astrological-
  lettrist imperial cosmology that defined Persianate courts for centuries after.
- **Patronage is explicitly transactional**: *Boon for the Khan* is a "royal boon"
  commissioned at a specific Qizilbash (Safavid Turkic military-elite) patron's request,
  with the patron's own preference (maximal popularization, made "quite literally
  stage-magical" for courtly/military life in Herat) shaping what got written and how
  openly.
- ʿAlī Ṣafī's father, Ḥusayn Vāʿiẓ Kāshifī (d. 1505) — Herati preacher, polymath,
  Naqshbandī Sufi — is credited with a broader project to create an "everyman's library"
  in Persian, foundational to Persianate courtly *and* popular culture through the
  colonial era. His *Qasimian Secrets* (*Asrār-i Qāsimī*) is the source *Boon for the
  Khan* condenses.
- Real institutional/political risk is treated as a live feature, not just a Ibn-Turka-
  specific anecdote: e.g. the Sufi poet Qāsim-i Anvār — a companion of Ibn Turka's in
  Cairo — was likewise exiled in 1427 for his lettrist associations.
- Comparative note: even Ronald Reagan is dropped in a footnote as having had "his court
  astrologer" — the paper frames the political use of occult science as a durable, not
  merely medieval, phenomenon.

## The Occult Quintet (*khamsa-yi muḥtajiba*)

Named via the mnemonic *kullu-hu sirr* ("the whole is a secret," K-L-H-S-R), given as an
explicit hierarchy from most elite/technically demanding to most accessible:

1. **kīmiyā** (alchemy) — elevating base metals to silver or gold.
2. **līmiyā** (talismanry) — conjoining active celestial forces with passive terrestrial
   ones to produce effects.
3. **hīmiyā** (subjugation) — persuading planets and compelling jinn via rigorous
   ritual, amuletic, or invocatory practice.
4. **sīmiyā** (illusionism) — projecting unreal images into observers' perception.
5. **rīmiyā** (trickery) — mixing terrestrial substances for astonishing effects.

*Boon for the Khan* focuses only on the bottom two (illusionism, trickery) — the most
"accessible" tier — with 12 operations each, drawn from Kāshifī's fuller *Qasimian
Secrets*, which covers all five.

## Sample operation categories (paraphrase only — see caution below)

Illusionism section alone covers: putrefactions (creating small magical creatures from
organic matter and animal blood, described with full step-by-step "recipes"),
suffumigations (burning specific mineral/organic compounds to produce smoke that reads
as giant figures or armies — one explicitly framed as a trick Plato once played on
Alexander the Great), food/drink endurance rites (preparations claimed to remove the
need to eat or drink for days/weeks), a wake-rite (to stay awake, or keep guests awake),
sleeper interrogation (extracting truthful answers from a sleeping subject), treasure
dowsing (using an animal to locate buried objects), predator taming, instant
agriculture, and "kohl-vision."

**Caution:** the specific English wording of these operations is Melvin-Koushki's own
translation and his copyrighted scholarly work. Any game text drawing on this material
should paraphrase and restructure substantially — treat this list as an index of
*categories* to research and reinterpret, not text to lift.

## Design relevance

- This is the primary court/political-economy source for the career-sim: patron
  relationships, commissioned works, popularization-vs-secrecy as a design axis, and
  institutional risk (inquisitions, rival scholars) are all directly attested here, not
  invented.
- The Occult Quintet is the most directly game-ready material found so far — a
  five-branch skill tree with real period names and an explicit prestige/cost ordering
  already baked in.
- The operation categories (putrefactions, suffumigations, endurance rites, etc.) are a
  strong model for ability/item flavor *categories* across all three prototypes, once
  reworded away from the source translation.
