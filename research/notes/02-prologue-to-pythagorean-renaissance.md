## Core move

Close reading of the prologue to Ibn Turka's *Investigations* (*Kitāb al-Mafāḥiṣ*, 1420)
as a case where, per the essay's Baroque motto, "form is content" — the prologue is
argued to be a literary-magical performance in its own right, not just introductory
throat-clearing. Ibn Turka is positioned as the pivotal figure of an "Occult-Scientific
Revolution" that is the Islamicate parallel to (and, per the author, source for) the
European Scientific Revolution story.

## Biography (the load-bearing facts)

- Full dates: 1369–1432. Chief Judge (qāḍī) of Isfahan.
- Humanist "champion of the Moderns over the Ancients"; known for defending the weak
  against the powerful in his judicial role.
- **Three state inquisitions**, engineered by jealous/vengeful colleagues. Won the first
  two. Lost the third — five years of wandering exile, died impoverished, in legal
  limbo.
- Mentor: Sayyid Ḥusayn Akhlāṭī (d. 1397), Cairo-based, hub of the "New Brethren of
  Purity" network (self-consciously named after the medieval Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ /
  "Brethren of Purity" behind the *Rasāʾil* encyclopedia and the *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm* /
  *Picatrix* tradition), radiating Cairo → Iran → India → Anatolia.
- Best friend and star student: Sharaf al-Dīn ʿAlī Yazdī (d. 1454), Timurid imperial
  historian and mathematician, worked at the Samarkand Observatory; likely copied Ibn
  Turka's own autograph manuscript (Tehran, Majlis Library MS 10196, ff. 52a–56a,
  copied/added to 1420–25).
- Patrons: Iskandar Sultan first (atelier famous for fusing star science, poetry, and
  book painting), then Bāysunghur (accomplished calligrapher, likely commissioner of the
  monumental Bāysunghur Qurʾan).
- Cairo companion Qāsim-i Anvār (d. 1434), a Sufi poet, was similarly exiled in 1427 over
  lettrist associations — a parallel persecution.
- Explicitly compared to Nicholas of Cusa (1401–1464, fellow lawyer, fellow
  Pythagoreanizer, but far less immediately influential) and, at length, to Giordano
  Bruno — both subordinated all contemporary schools of thought to a totalizing
  Pythagorean-lettrist code, both were "historiographically burnt at the stake"
  (Bruno literally; Ibn Turka's *Mafāḥiṣ* remains understudied/"on the Index").

## Cosmology (the load-bearing concepts)

- **Lettrism** (*ʿilm al-ḥurūf*) = "Islamic kabbalah": treats Arabic letters, especially
  the Qurʾan's mysterious opening letters (*muqaṭṭaʿāt*), as mathematical-cosmic units.
  *Investigations* is called the most philosophically systematic formulation of lettrism
  ever written.
- **The Ṭahawī Circle**: *Investigations*'s central diagram, called the first "modern"
  lettrist version of the Pythagorean Tetractys.
- **Planet–Pearl–Peach**: the book's own structure — three "Globes of Light"
  corresponding to Mental / Written / Spoken registers of the Letter, organized as an
  ascent (up to Planet) → descent (down to Pearl) → ascent-again (up through Peach)
  journey. A fourth part discusses the Letter itself as form-as-content.
- Two named sources only: Ibn ʿArabī (1165–1240, Andalusian "Supreme Master") and Saʿd
  al-Dīn Ḥamūya (d. 1252, his "Iranian twin") — Ibn Turka is credited as the first to
  pair them as co-founders of lettrism, after ʿAlī (cousin/son-in-law of the Prophet,
  identified with Pythagoras-reborn in this tradition).
- Coincides in time (1420) with the construction of Ulugh Beg's Samarkand Observatory;
  its star tables were the most mathematically precise pre-telescopic tables produced,
  used from the Strait of Malacca to London into the 19th century.
- Seven-tier epistemic hierarchy from Ibn Turka's Persian treatise *On the Splitting of
  the Moon and the Last Hour*: traditionist literalism (bottom) → ... → lettrism (top,
  apex of human perfection). Only theologians, philosophers, Sufis, and lettrists are
  named here, in ascending order — the full seven-tier breakdown is cited onward to a
  *different* Melvin-Koushki article, "Selenocentrism and Heliocentrism" (not in hand).
- Talismanic architecture: Safavid New Isfahan and the Shaykh Luṭf Allāh Mosque read as
  built diagrams of the Planet-Pearl-Peach cosmology.
- Ibn Turka's own epigraph, used as the paper's second epigraph: "The more a thing is
  occulted, the more it is made manifest."
