# 40 Choices: A Narrative Designer's Report on Ibn Turka's Life

**Prepared for the TurkaGame visual novel.** Methodology note before the list: this
report follows the same move Matthew Melvin-Koushki himself uses in "Dr Dee's Ottoman
Adventure" — take a real, documented life and ask what a different decision at a real
juncture would have meant. Every entry below is tagged with how grounded it is:

- **ATTESTED** — the historical record documents this juncture directly (a real
  decision, relationship, or turning point named in the sources).
- **PLAUSIBLE-GAP** — a real, documented life-transition where the sources don't
  record the decision-making itself; the choice fills a genuine gap with something a
  person in Ibn Turka's position plausibly faced.
- **INVENTED-COMPATIBLE** — not attested at all, but consistent with the attested
  world and useful for pacing/characterization (a scene needs a decision beat here
  even where history is silent).

Full biographical/cosmological grounding: [docs/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md](../../docs/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md).
This document does not yet specify game mechanics — see the open questions at the
bottom and the conversation that follows.

## Act I — Cairo & Formation

**1. Which teacher to seek out first.** ATTESTED — Ibn Turka "travel[ed] far and wide
and seek[ing] out many authorities" before Cairo. *Choice: pursue rigorous scholastic
training in an established madrasa first, or go looking for unconventional teachers
immediately.* Sets the player's starting orientation toward orthodoxy vs. heterodoxy.

**2. How openly to align with Akhlati.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — the text calls Sayyid Husayn
Akhlati "infamous"; public discipleship under him was a reputational risk even before
any inquisition. *Choice: declare discipleship openly, or study quietly and keep the
association deniable.* Early seed for how much risk the player is willing to carry
for genuine study.

**3. How to treat the friendship with Yazdi.** ATTESTED — described as "my Brother in
God," an unusually warm bond for the genre. *Choice: collaborate as equals, sharing
half-formed ideas freely, or mentor him at arm's length, guarding the emerging
system.* Determines whether Yazdi becomes a true creative partner or a loyal
subordinate — matters later when he's the one entrusted with the surviving
manuscripts.

**4. How close to stand with Qasim-i Anvar.** ATTESTED — a Cairo companion, later
exiled in 1427 for the same lettrist associations. *Choice: bond deeply as
fellow-travelers, or keep him a respected acquaintance at a safe distance.* A choice
whose cost isn't visible until Act VII.

**5. Which lineage to foreground.** ATTESTED — Ibn Turka is unusual for insisting on
pairing Ibn 'Arabi *and* Sa'd al-Din Hamuya as co-founders of lettrism, rather than
picking one. *Choice: commit fully to Ibn 'Arabi's more famous, more scrutinized
tradition, elevate the less-studied Hamuya instead, or insist on both (the historical
choice) at the cost of pleasing neither camp's partisans.*

## Act II — First Patron: Iskandar Sultan

**6. Accept patronage at all.** ATTESTED — Iskandar Sultan is documented as the first
of three Timurid sovereigns Ibn Turka sought to cultivate. *Choice: accept the
court appointment, or remain an independent, unaffiliated scholar living more
precariously but more freely.*

**7. How much doctrine to reveal to a young, ambitious patron.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP.
*Choice: present the full, still-forming lettrist system, or a court-palatable,
watered-down version that won't spook conservative advisors.*

**8. Align with the atelier's artists, or stand apart as a jurist-philosopher.**
ATTESTED — Iskandar Sultan's atelier was known for fusing astronomy, poetry, and
manuscript painting. *Choice: actively collaborate with the court poets and painters
on shared cosmological imagery, or keep to philosophy and law, leaving the art to
others.*

**9. React to the first flicker of rival jealousy.** ATTESTED (pattern) — all three
later inquisitions are described as "contrived by jealous and vengeful colleagues."
*Choice: flaunt the novel theory to build reputation fast, or introduce it
cautiously, letting it spread by reputation rather than declaration.*

**10. Loyalty through political risk.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — Timurid succession politics
were genuinely volatile in this period. *Choice: stay loyal to Iskandar Sultan
through a downturn in his fortunes, or quietly begin cultivating the next patron
early.*

## Act III — Second Patron: Baysunghur

**11. How to present himself anew at Baysunghur's court.** ATTESTED — both patrons
are documented; the transition itself is not detailed in the sources in hand.
*Choice: lead with the calligraphic/manuscript-arts affinity (Baysunghur was himself
an accomplished calligrapher), or lead with astronomical/mathematical prestige
(Samarkand was rising in the same years).*

**12. Whether to work on the Baysunghur Qur'an.** ATTESTED — Baysunghur was likely
its commissioner. *Choice: offer a lettrist framing for the calligraphy program
(numerology of the sacred text made visible), or stay deliberately separate from a
project that risky theological scrutiny could attach to.*

**13. Balance judiciary duties against court philosophy.** ATTESTED (the judgeship
itself) — *Choice: let the qadi post lapse into a formality while the philosophical
project consumes his time, or keep genuinely active on the bench even as court
prestige grows.*

**14. Pursue a formal tie to the Samarkand Observatory.** ATTESTED — Yazdi worked
there. *Choice: actively seek association with Ulugh Beg's circle through Yazdi, or
stay Isfahan-rooted and let the connection remain informal, through friendship alone.*

**15. Respond to a rival's first public challenge.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — sets up the
first inquisition. *Choice: answer publicly and immediately, risking escalation, or
let it pass, risking the appearance of having no answer.*

## Act IV — Choosing the Sciences (the Occult Quintet)

**16. Primary specialization.** ATTESTED (the historical Ibn Turka's actual choice
was lettrism/*himiya*-adjacent theory) — *Choice: pick a primary science among
kīmiyā, līmiyā, hīmiyā, sīmiyā, rīmiyā (see
[docs/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md](../../docs/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md#the-occult-sciences-as-a-structured-hierarchy)).*
This is the mechanical skill-tree entry point.

**17. Specialize narrowly or diversify.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: pursue lettrism
exclusively as the historical Ibn Turka effectively did, or diversify into
astrology/alchemy alongside it for steadier patronage income.*

**18. Mathematical foundation first, or theurgic ambition first.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP —
*Choice: build rigorous mathematical/astronomical grounding before advancing
theurgic claims, or lead with the bold cosmological claims and backfill the math.*

**19. When to go public with the sensory-theory innovations.** ATTESTED — his "new
sensory theory" boldly overturns Aristotelian-Avicennan hierarchy. *Choice: publish
early to stake the claim, risking scandal before his reputation can absorb it, or
hold it back for years until his position is secure.*

**20. When to finalize and circulate Investigations' core diagram.** ATTESTED —
composed 1420, the same year Samarkand Observatory construction began. *Choice: rush
to publish alongside the observatory's own prestige moment, or take the years needed
to refine the Ṭahawī Circle privately first.*

## Act V — Popularizer or Secret-Keeper

**21. Register of the masterwork.** ATTESTED — the real Investigations is famously
"ontologically ornate," elitist. *Choice: write in that dense, elite register (the
historical choice), or aim for something closer to Kashifi's later "Everyman's
Library" accessibility — a real, different path attested elsewhere in the same
tradition.*

**22. Teach widely or keep the circle small.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP (New Brethren of Purity
model implies a bounded circle) — *Choice: actively recruit and train students
broadly, or keep the New Brethren small, trusted, and hard to infiltrate.*

**23. Write vernacular popularizations alongside the Arabic masterwork.** PLAUSIBLE-
GAP, modeled on the real *Boon for the Khan* popularizing move a generation later.
*Choice: produce simplified Persian versions for wider court/military reach, or
refuse — the ideas stand or fall in their full difficulty.*

**24. Handle requests for simplified, practical grimoire content.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP —
*Choice: take the income and status of writing down to a patron's practical request,
or decline and protect the work's prestige at a real financial cost.*

**25. How fully to name his sources.** ATTESTED — he cites only Ibn 'Arabi and
Hamuya, insisting on unprecedentedness elsewhere. *Choice: be generous crediting the
tradition he draws on, or claim more originality than is strictly true — a choice
with real consequences for how rivals can attack him later.*

## Act VI — The Bench: Judge of Isfahan

**26. Accept the Chief Judge appointment at all.** ATTESTED — his actual, documented
day job. *Choice: take the post (the historical choice) and gain legal standing and
income, or decline it to remain a purer, more precarious scholar-only identity.*

**27. A defining case.** ATTESTED (reputation) — "known for his fierce defense of the
weak against the powerful." *Choice: rule for a powerful patron's interest in a
contested case, or rule for the weaker party at real political cost — the choice
that built his historical reputation.*

**28. Occult reasoning on the bench.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: keep total separation
between "judge" and "occultist" personas, or let lettrist/numerical reasoning
visibly inform a judicial argument, a genuinely dangerous rhetorical move.*

**29. A case touching a rival occultist.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: show scholarly
solidarity and mercy to an accused sorcerer, recognizing a kindred practice, or
enforce orthodoxy strictly to protect his own position from association.*

**30. Where peak years go.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: prioritize the steady
legitimacy of the bench, or prioritize the philosophical writing at the height of
his powers, letting judicial duties slide.*

## Act VII — Three Inquisitions

**31. First inquisition: mode of defense.** ATTESTED — he won the first two. *Choice:
mount a rigorous legal/rhetorical defense, call in patron favor, or attempt both at
once (riskier to coordinate, stronger if it works).*

**32. First inquisition: recant or hold firm.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: offer a
partial, face-saving recantation to placate accusers, or hold every position without
concession.*

**33. Second inquisition: distance from associates, or not.** ATTESTED (context) —
Qasim-i Anvar was exiled in 1427 for the same associations. *Choice: preemptively
distance himself from Qasim-i Anvar and others to protect his own position, or
refuse to abandon anyone, accepting the shared risk.* This is where Act I's choice
\#4 pays off or costs the most.

**34. Third inquisition: bend the knee.** ATTESTED — the single most dramatic
documented fact of his life: he "would refuse to bend the knee during his three
inquisitions, despite the danger and punishing consequences." *Choice: bend — survive
compromised, publicly diminished, his system publicly renounced in whole or part — or
hold firm, as the historical Ibn Turka did, and lose everything.* This is the
game's central dramatic hinge; a VN honest to its source material should make holding
firm the hard, costly, but earned choice rather than the obviously "correct" one.

**35. Third inquisition: flee, or stand trial.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: attempt to
flee before judgment (uncertain, dangerous, no guarantee of a warmer reception
elsewhere), or stand and face the tribunal directly.*

## Act VIII — Exile & Legacy

**36. Destination in exile.** ATTESTED that five years of wandering exile happened;
PLAUSIBLE-GAP on where. *Choice: seek a new patron at a rival court (Aqquyunlu?
further east?), retreat into pure private scholarship with no court at all, or
attempt reconciliation and return.*

**37. Keep working, or go dark.** PLAUSIBLE-GAP — *Choice: continue writing and
teaching in exile, vulnerable but productive, or go silent to protect what remains
of the work and his safety.*

**38. Entrust the manuscripts to Yazdi.** ATTESTED (strongly implied) — Yazdi likely
copied the surviving autograph collection (Tehran, Majlis Library MS 10196).
*Choice: formally entrust his life's papers to Yazdi for safekeeping and eventual
transmission, or keep them close and risk them being lost with him.* Pays off (or
doesn't) Act I's choice \#3.

**39. How to face death.** ATTESTED (Melvin-Koushki's own framing) — draws the
explicit parallel to Bruno, "martyr vindicated after death." *Choice: die reconciled
and at peace with the cost, embittered and railing against his accusers, or
defiantly triumphant, certain of eventual vindication.*

**40. The final testament.** INVENTED-COMPATIBLE — a closing scene beat the sources
don't attest to directly, needed for a satisfying ending. *Choice: leave a public,
defiant final statement for the wider scholarly world, or a quiet, private
transmission of the actual texts to the New Brethren only — visibility vs.
survival of the work itself, one last time.*
