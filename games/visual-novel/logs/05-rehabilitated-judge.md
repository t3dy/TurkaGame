# The Rehabilitated Judge — survival by other means

> Bends at the third inquisition, but a career of ruling for the weak from a real judgeship survives the scandal.

## Act I — Cairo & Formation

*You have come to Cairo the way water finds a crack: because everywhere else was closed to you. The city is full of teachers. Most of them are wrong.*

### 1. Which teacher to seek out first  `c01` · ATTESTED · theme: knowledge

Half a dozen names circulate in the study-circles of Cairo, each with disciples who swear the others are frauds. You have limited time and less money. Somewhere in this city is a teacher worth following into real danger for — you just have to guess right before you can be sure.

**→ Chose: Rigorous scholastic training first**
> Formal madrasa training — orthodox, respected, and no faster route into Akhlati's more radical circle.

*Also available:* Seek unconventional teachers immediately

*The madrasa disciplines your mind before it frees it. A slower start, a sturdier one.*

### 2. How openly to align with Akhlati  `c02` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: loyalty

Sayyid Ḥusayn Akhlātī draws the kind of student who does not advertise the association afterward. He is called brilliant by the same people who call him dangerous, often in the same breath. Attaching your name to his is not a neutral act.

**→ Chose: Declare discipleship openly**
> Everyone will know you study under Akhlati — respected by his admirers, flagged by his enemies.

*Also available:* Study quietly, keep it deniable

*People now know exactly whose student you are. Some respect it. Some are already keeping a file.*

### 3. How to treat the friendship with Yazdi  `c03` · ATTESTED · theme: loyalty

Sharaf al-Dīn ʿAlī of Yazd listens the way few people do — not waiting for his turn to speak, but actually turning an idea over before handing it back sharper. You could keep him at the respectful distance a teacher keeps a promising student, or let him all the way in.

**→ Chose: Collaborate as equals**
> Treat Yazdi, your sharpest student, as a genuine partner, not a subordinate.

*Also available:* Mentor him at arm's length

*Yazdi becomes something closer to a co-author than a student. It will matter later, in ways neither of you can see yet.*

### 4. How close to stand with Qasim-i Anvar  `c04` · ATTESTED · theme: loyalty

Qāsim-i Anvār has the easy warmth of someone who has never yet had to choose between a friend and his own safety. You like him. You do not yet know what liking him will cost.

**→ Chose: Bond deeply as fellow-travelers**
> Bond deeply with Qasim-i Anvar. Real loyalty — and his risks become yours.

*Also available:* Keep a respectful distance

*You and Qāsim-i Anvār become the kind of friends who would vouch for each other under oath. That vow has not been tested yet.*

### 5. Which lineage to foreground  `c05` · ATTESTED · theme: knowledge

Ibn ʿArabī's name opens doors and closes others just as fast; everyone has an opinion of the Andalusian mystic. Ḥamūya is quieter, less argued-over, and — you increasingly suspect — just as essential. No one before you has thought to need them both.

**→ Chose: Commit to Ibn 'Arabi's tradition**
> Foreground Ibn 'Arabi alone — the famous, fiercely contested half of the tradition.

*Also available:* Elevate Hamuya instead · Insist on both (the historical choice)

*You lean into the more famous, more scrutinized half of your inheritance.*

## Act II — First Patron: Iskandar Sultan

*A summons arrives, sealed and unhurried, the way princes write when they already expect yes. Iskandar Sultan is building a court where astronomers sit beside poets. You are being asked to sit somewhere in that room.*

The offer from Iskandar Sultan's court is generous, and generosity from princes is never free. Independence means going hungry on your own terms. Patronage means eating well on someone else's.

**→ Chose: Accept the court appointment**

*Also available:* Remain independent

*You take the court appointment. Security, visibility, and a patron whose fortunes are now entangled with yours.*

### 7. How much doctrine to reveal to Iskandar Sultan  `c07` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: knowledge

Iskandar Sultan is young, curious, and not stupid — a dangerous combination in a patron, because he will notice if you are performing for him rather than teaching him. He asks what you actually believe.

**→ Chose: Present the full, still-forming system**
> Show Iskandar Sultan the full, unabridged system. Impressive — and now he knows all of it.

*Also available:* A court-palatable, watered-down version

*The prince gets the real system, unabridged. He is impressed, and a little unsettled, and does not forget either reaction.*

### 8. Align with the atelier's artists, or stand apart  `c08` · ATTESTED · theme: power

### 9. React to the first flicker of rival jealousy  `c09` · ATTESTED · theme: power

A colleague has started repeating your ideas back to you at gatherings, half-credited, testing to see if you'll correct him in public. It is the first small skirmish of a war that has not been declared yet.

**→ Chose: Flaunt the theory to build reputation fast**

### 10. Loyalty as Shah Rukh moves against Iskandar Sultan  `c10` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: loyalty

Word reaches you plainly, the way this kind of word always does: Shah Rukh has moved against his own nephew. Iskandar Sultan will be blinded, then executed. You are known, by name, as his man — and Shah Rukh's own son, Bāysunghur, is the only safe court left standing nearby.

**→ Chose: Stay loyal as he is blinded and executed**
> Stand visibly by Iskandar Sultan as Shah Rukh destroys him. Reckless now, unforgettable if you survive it.

*Also available:* Distance yourself before the executioner arrives

*You stay visibly loyal to a man about to lose his eyes and then his life. Reckless by any sane measure — and if you survive it, no one will ever doubt where you stood.*

### 15. Respond to a rival's first public challenge  `c15` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: power

A rival scholar stands up at a gathering and challenges your framework directly, by name, in front of people whose opinions matter. The room waits to see if you'll answer now or later.

**→ Chose: Answer publicly and immediately**

## Act IV — Choosing the Sciences

*You have read enough, listened enough, argued enough. The five sciences lie open in front of you like five roads out of the same city. A life is not long enough to walk them all.*

### 16. Primary specialization  `c16` · ATTESTED · theme: knowledge

The Occult Quintet is not a menu — it is closer to five separate lives, each demanding a lifetime of study to do honestly. Kīmiyā transforms matter. Līmiyā binds the heavens to the earth. Hīmiyā compels what does not want to be compelled. Sīmiyā shapes what people believe they see. Rīmiyā simply astonishes. Choose which life.

**→ Chose: Kimiya (alchemy)**
> Kimiya, alchemy — the most elite science, the slowest to pay off.

*Also available:* Limiya (talismanry) · Himiya (subjugation) · Simiya (illusionism) · Rimiya (trickery)

*Skill:* +3 kimiya

*Kīmiyā — the most elite and demanding of the five. Slow, expensive, and unmistakably prestigious when it works.*

### 17. Specialize narrowly or diversify  `c17` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: knowledge

Mastery of one science, deep and undiluted, is the historical path — and the harder one. Diversifying pays better, faster, and thinner.

**→ Chose: Pursue the primary science exclusively**
> Commit to one science alone — the harder, historical path.

*Also available:* Diversify for steadier income

*You commit to one science and let it define you completely — the historical path, and the harder one to walk back from.*

### 18. Mathematical foundation first, or theurgic ambition first  `c18` · PLAUSIBLE-GAP · theme: knowledge

*Also available:* Lead with bold cosmological claims

*Skill:* +1 kimiya

## Ending — The Rehabilitated Judge

You bent the knee, and it cost you your grandest ambitions. But the reputation you built defending the weak from the bench outlasted the scandal — you survive, specifically, as a judge.

Qasim-i Anvar was the friend you chose, in Cairo, to bond with deeply — and the one you distanced yourself from when the tribunal asked. He never said so aloud. He also never fully forgave it. You never diversified. Whatever is remembered of your work will be remembered as the work of one science, pursued as far as a single lifetime could take it. You died reconciled to what it all cost — which is more than most of the men who tried you managed. Your final statement circulated openly — copied, argued with, condemned, and therefore preserved. It was kīmiyā that shaped your reputation in the end — the patient, expensive science, and the patience it taught leaks into everything else they say about you.
