    The five rules
    Every rule reads the letter’s own body, so there is nothing to memorise: look at the glyph. The marks on each tile show what is there — dots and which side they sit, whether the form closes, whether it drops below the line.

    The Letter Machine

    A letter here is a verb, and which verb is written in the letter’s own body rather than in a table. That is the whole design: nine letters bind because they enclose, seventeen pour because they drop below the line, twelve raise and three lower according to which side their dots sit, two hold an axis because they are a single upright stroke — and two, dāl and kāf, do nothing at all, because nothing is true of them. Finding the rule is the game; the rule covers the alphabet.

    Where this comes from, and where it does not

    PORTAL The abjad values and the ladder they form — 1 to 10, then tens, then hundreds — are the series as the tradition numbers it (portal entry abjad-numerology). Raise and lower move a value one rung of that series, not by one unit, because the series is the thing being asserted.

    INTERPRETATION The form facts on each tile are observable properties of the written glyph, generated and checked in games/abjad-tower/build_letters.py. But what each form does — closed binds, tail pours — is ours. The idea that a letter’s shakl synthesises its properties is REPORTED from al-Būnī in this project’s design brief, citing a source not held in this repository. So no rule here claims to be his.

    PORTAL That alif is singular — a line rather than an ingredient, taking no alif beside it — rests on alif = 1 and on its standing as the avatar of divine Oneness.

    Transposition

    Nothing is created. A letter read as matter is the same relation in another representation, and which element it turns out to be is decided by a scheme that is hidden from you — the same three rival schemes that decide which letters hold each other up in Abjad Tower. So a transposition here and a tower there are two experiments on one question, and they share the notebook below. A transposition that fails is as informative as one that succeeds.
