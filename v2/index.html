The prototypes

Everything here runs on one engine. Eight instructions are derived from facts you can check against an Arabic grammar — closed forms bind, tailed forms pour, dots raise or lower, an upright stroke holds a frame, and the six letters that never join what follows are where a word breaks. Five historical rulesets genuinely disagree about which of those a letter may use. Preview and execution are the same function, so nothing here can show you a consequence it will not then deliver.

Every app can be drawn in two hands — Lapis, the dark table, or Ink, the engraved page with hatched shading and the elements drawn as the barred triangles they are. The toggle is in each app’s topbar and the choice sticks. Collapses now play out in time, and what you watch is the fall the engine computed, replayed step by step.

Games

Each has a moment-to-moment verb that is not writing, so that writing stays scarce. Every level is solver-checked before a person sees it, and checked a second way for whether the letters actually matter.

The Pushing Floor

block-pusher · 2 floors · solver-checked

Walk and shove stones onto marks — until you meet a stone you can never get behind, and have to write a letter to change what “one stone” means. A closed letter binds two stones into one body.

Arrow keys or WASD to walk · click a letter, then a cell beside you. Verified: neither floor is solvable without its letter.

The Standing Word

stacker · 2 levels · solver-checked

Build with gravity switched off, then let it in. A rāʾ at the outboard end is joined to nothing and goes; the same rāʾ inboard carries everything hanging off it. The letter is not good or bad — its position is.

Click a letter, then a cell on the ground or beside something standing. Dashed arrows show exactly what would fall.

The Reckoner

four mechanics · each checked its own way

Acting on what already stands. Take a letter out and see what survives — but a letter resting on the ground cannot be taken out at all, so it has to be carried by the word. Name a number and every run summing to it comes apart. Work out which of the five metaphysics you are standing in without being told. And find the direction from which a body of letters reads as a word.

Two are recovered from v1 with better grounding than they had; one takes back the thing v1 did better than v2; one rebuilds Yūsuf Ascent’s perspective puzzle out of language.

The Descent

roguelike · 5 floors · every floor checked under every metaphysics

A run of floors, and what varies between runs is the metaphysics. Below the surface each floor runs one of the four historical lettrisms, dealt in a seeded order and never named. Your letters behave slightly differently than they did upstairs; work out where you are before you let gravity in, or commit on a guess and learn from the fall.

Compose a word, click where it starts, let gravity in. Name the metaphysics for a candle; spend candles on probes. A collapse costs a life and teaches for free.

The Unmaking

comparison · 3 routes · open question

v2 could build and could not unbuild. Rather than invent a primitive to close that gap, here are three candidates on the same structure — one tightly sourced, one admitted fiction, and one with no claim at all, which the other two have to beat.

Click a letter to aim, pick a route, or press Try all three. This one wants your judgement, not just your attention.

The Glyphs

artwork · 26 signs · both hands

The four elements, the three principles, the seven planets and the twelve signs, each drawn as a path rather than typed as a character — so nothing renders as a box, and every sign takes the hand it is drawn in. The first artwork this project has made itself, and the sheet the Golden Dawn build will draw its blocks from.

Toggle the hand to see the same forms on the night table and on the engraved page. Labelled as my drawings of the standard forms, not reproductions.

The Scriptorium

letter IDE · 4 tasks · five metaphysics

Compose a program of letters and watch what it does to the world. Switch the ruleset and the same letters behave differently: Sufi lettrism refuses to sever at all, so مرم is one body there and two elsewhere. The world can also be read back — including the parts you did not write.

Evidence is always shown; the rule is earned. You learn what an operation is by watching a letter do it, never from a panel.

What none of this claims. No person has played any of it. Every number in every README comes from a solver, a --verify, or a scripted replay through the real input path. Nothing here is asserted to be fun, balanced or well-paced — and two levels per mode is a demonstration of a mechanic, not a game with a curve.
