# MUGHAL.md — The Indo-Timurid Apotheosis

**Grimoire page, 2026-09-02.** Grounded in *OC*, *Prologue*, research/notes/01. ⚠ not
held: "Timurid-Mughal Philosopher-Kings as Sultan-Scientists," in *Rulers as Authors
in the Islamic World* (Brill, 2024), 596–630 — citation exact from the *Prologue*
bibliography.

## The claim

The Mughals — "Indo-Timurids" in MK's insistent usage — carried the Timurid occult
platform to its imperial maximum: Akbar as "the most successful *avatar* of this
millennial occult-scientific sovereignty model, syncretizing Muslim, Hindu, Jain, and
Christian traditions into 'modern India'" (notes/01); the Tāj Maḥal as the platform's
architectural apotheosis alongside New Isfahan (*OC*, *Prologue*).

## Fixed points (held)

- **The naming argument**: "Indo-Timurid (Mughal)" (*OC*) — the dynasty as Timurid
  continuation, which makes Ibn Turka's platform their *patrimony*, not an import.
- **The Tāj as machine** (*Prologue*): a "glorious mausoleum marrying Sun to Moon for
  Underworld to Overworld journeying — … now a tourist trap, so no longer a legible
  machine for achieving out-of-body philosophy, much less a computer for making
  Paradise on Earth." Legibility-loss as the colonial wound, in one sentence.
- **The Deccan bridge** (*OC*): ʿAlī ʿĀdilshāh's *Nujūm al-ʿulūm* — "the most
  extensively and gorgeously illustrated of all early modern Persian books, and
  almost exclusively imperial and occult," with "innovative emphasis on magic war
  tech," whose Greek-India/Persian-Sanskrit marriage "helped inspire the ʿAdilshahis'
  Mughal foes to do the same in turn."
- **Kāshifī in the colonial classroom** (*OC* n.8): *Anvār-i Suhaylī* "assigned as a
  textbook to British colonial agents in Mughal India" — the platform's texts became
  the colonizer's primers, then the colonizer buried the platform.
