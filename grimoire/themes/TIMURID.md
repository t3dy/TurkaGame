## The claim

The Timurid fifteenth century is where the occult-imperial synthesis was *built*:
"it is only in the long fifteenth century that these grimoires came to be
incorporated into a coherent system constructed for Timurid imperial purposes,
inherited by, elaborated upon and more systematically deployed by the Safavids,
Ottomans, Indo-Timurids (Mughals) and other competing Persianate polities in turn"
(*OC*). Ibn Turka with Yazdī "created the astrological-lettrist platform that was to
define Persianate imperial cosmology for the next several centuries."

## Fixed points (held)

- **1420 as hinge year** (*Prologue*, BIOGRAPHY): *Investigations* completed; the
  Samarkand Observatory begun; its tables "so unprecedentedly mathematically precise
  they were used everywhere from the Strait of Malacca to London to the nineteenth
  century."
- **The patron trio as institutional spectrum** — Iskandar Sulṭān (atelier
  experimentalism: "star science, poetry and book painting"), Bāysunghur (the
  kitābkhāna; the monumental Bāysunghur Qurʾan, fragments still selling "for
  eyewatering sums (up to £500,000)"), Ulugh Beg (the observatory) — one science,
  three apparatuses (*Prologue* n.34).
- **Kāshifī as popularizing bookend** (*OC*): the everyman's library that carried the
  Timurid synthesis into every later court — and into British colonial classrooms.
- **Total aesthetic adoption** by Safavid/Mughal/Ottoman/Uzbek successors (Lentz &
  Lowry, cited *OC* n.10): the century sets the continuum's style sheet.
