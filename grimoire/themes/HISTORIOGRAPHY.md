# HISTORIOGRAPHY.md — Astrolettrist History-Writing, and Writing About It

**Grimoire page, 2026-09-02.** Grounded in *Prologue* (Yazdī; metahistory), *OC*
(Kemālpaşazāde title), *Meanings*. Two subjects deliberately on one page: how the
period wrote history, and how MK writes it — because his method mirrors his sources.

## The claim, part 1 — the period's

History-writing itself became an occult science: Yazdī, "Timurid imperial historian
and mathematician par excellence," was "the first to make the science of history
fully formally astrolettrist" (*Prologue* n.36) — chronology computed, conjunctions
read, the dynasty's story written as the stars' story. ⚠ Kemālpaşazāde's "lettrist
call" chapter (title held) extends the mode to Ottoman conquest-history; "In Defense
of Geomancy" shows Yazdī defending divinatory method as historical method's cousin.

## The claim, part 2 — MK's own

MK's essays *perform* what they describe (declared, not accidental): "metahistory is
structurally often weirdly literary too" (*Prologue*); coincidences are read as
"metahistorically poetically perfect" (the observatory and the summa in 1420;
Ḥamūya's son converting the Mongols by talisman; Isfahan/Naples both at 200,000).
The style — Joycean portmanteaus, "swarwords," epigraphs from Finnegans Wake — is a
methodological wager: that ornate form can carry analytic content, exactly as his
subjects held. *Meanings*: "stories are spells."

## Why this matters for us

- **Reading MK requires two registers at once**: extract the checkable claim
  (manuscript, date, census) AND register the performance. Our grimoire pages quote
  the performances *as* performances and cite the checkables separately — keep that
  discipline.
- **The game already imitates the period mode**: the Chronicle voice writing the
  run's history one inked line at a time is astrolettrist historiography in
  miniature — the player's Yazdī. The witness system (a chronicle copied, annotated,
  corrected by later hands) is manuscript transmission enacted.

## What it gives the game

- Yazdī deserves promotion from companion to **co-author of the record**: an ending
  variant where the chronicle the player has watched being inked is revealed as
  Yazdī's hand (the autograph note *Prologue* n.36 supports it: the Majlis MS is
  "almost certainly by the hand of… Yazdī"). Cheap, true, devastating.
- A `readings/` practice rule: when the narrative designer reads a *primary*
  performance (like the translated Mafāḥiṣ prologue), the reading must separate
  three layers — what it says, what it does, what we can use — mirroring this page.
