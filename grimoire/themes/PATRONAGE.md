# PATRONAGE.md — What the Occult Sciences Teach About Patronage, and Vice Versa

**Grimoire page, 2026-09-02.** Grounded in full local texts of Melvin-Koushki's
*Prologue to Pythagorean Renaissance* (IHIW, 2025; hereafter *Prologue*), *The Occult
Court* (Al-Masāq, 2025; *OC*) and *The Meanings of Islamic Magic* (MRW, 2026;
*Meanings*), plus docs/BIOGRAPHY.md and research/notes/01–03. Claims from MK works not
held locally are marked ⚠.

## The claim

In the post-Mongol Persianate world, occult science and patronage were **not two
systems but one**: the sciences supplied the cosmology, legitimation, prognostication
and spectacle that courts ran on, and courts supplied the observatories, ateliers,
salaries and protection the sciences ran on. *OC* states it at maximum compression:
"Political magic: few terms are more redundant." Occult scientists "often served as
political scientists too, their numbers waxing and waning with the waxing and waning
of empires."

## The evidence in hand

- **Ibn Turka's own three-patron arc** (BIOGRAPHY; *Prologue* n.34): Iskandar Sulṭān's
  atelier "celebrated to this day for combining star science, poetry and book
  painting"; Bāysunghur "an accomplished calligrapher in his own right" and likely
  commissioner of the monumental Bāysunghur Qurʾan; Ulugh Beg, whose observatory and
  star-tables Ibn Turka's circle fed — "the one patron whose programme was his own
  programme" (BIOGRAPHY). Patronage is not generic favor: each patron is a distinct
  *demand profile* with a distinct apparatus attached.
- **The commission as instrument** (*OC*): *Boon for the Khan* exists because a
  Qizilbash governor, Durmish Khān Shāmlū, ordered a working occultist to extract,
  decode and localize his father's grimoire — specifying reproducibility ("ingredients
  that can be clearly identified and reliably locally sourced") and audience
  ("comprehensible and useful to everyone"). A patron's commission sets scope,
  cipher-level, and even epistemology.
- **Protection and its price**: Ibn Turka's tribunals were "contrived by jealous and
  vengeful colleagues" (*Prologue*); patrons could delay but not dissolve that
  machinery, and Qāsim-i Anvār — "cancelled and exiled in 1427 for his lettrist
  associations" (*OC* n.24) — shows the network-liability side of the same coin.
- **The mutual-capture structure** (*OC* on ʿAlī Ṣafī's *Amulet of Protection*): the
  grimoire tradition itself theorizes patronage from below — "psychic control of
  tyrants and persuasiveness with rulers" offered to bureaucrats as standard product.
  MK dubs it "occult democracy": the client arms himself against the patron.

## Contested / open

Günther & Pielow's response (portal corpus, read in head only) pushes back on how far
"science" should stretch; the patronage thesis itself is less contested than the
epistemic one. ⚠ MK's fullest patronage statements are in works not held locally:
"Powers of One" (IHIW 2017), "How to Rule the World" (JPS 2018), "Early Modern
Islamicate Empire" (Wiley-Blackwell 2018), *The Occult Science of Empire in
Aqquyunlu-Safavid Iran* (forthcoming). Citations exact (from bibliographies in held
texts); contents unverified.

## What it gives the game

- CareerSim's patron system is validated in shape (demand profiles, contracts,
  expectation inflation) and indicted in depth: the sources show patrons specifying
  *epistemology and cipher-level*, which no contract field yet captures. Candidate:
  contract terms that constrain HOW a deliverable may be written (encoded vs. plain —
  the Kashifian Cipher as a mechanic; see [GRIMOIRESASCOURTLYMANUALS.md](GRIMOIRESASCOURTLYMANUALS.md)).
- "Occult democracy" is an unmined encounter family: the *client's* countermagic.
  A vizier asks Turka for the operation that manages the prince.
- The retainer obligation (already built) should differ by patron: Iskandar's
  experimentalism, Bāysunghur's book-luxury, Ulugh Beg's proof-or-nothing — three
  glosses, one mechanic.
