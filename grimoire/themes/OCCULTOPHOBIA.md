# OCCULTOPHOBIA.md — The Bias Against Occult Science in Ottoman and Persian Studies

**Grimoire page, 2026-09-02.** Grounded in full local *Meanings* (MK's own field
memoir of the bias), *Prologue* (the Ibn Turka case), the Günther–Pielow response
(portal corpus, head), and the *Treasures of Knowledge* TOC (portal corpus). ⚠ marks
unverified attributions.

## The claim

Ottoman, Persian and broader Islamic studies inherited — and their **archives
institutionalized** — a classification of occult science as "Bad Religion and Never
Science," producing a field where the *majority practice* of the period's learned
elite is treated as embarrassing marginalia. MK's blunt census (*Meanings*): "every
major surviving and accessible archive internationally classes Islamic Magic as Bad
Religion and Never Science in high nineteenth-century pith-helmeted fashion."

## The mechanisms (held texts)

- **Missionary → bureaucratic pipeline**: Zwemer's "Islam Is Magic" polemic taken up
  by Glidden (State Department → Library of Congress), who made it "the official
  classification principle for all Islamic cultures under Anglo-American imperialism"
  (*Meanings*). The bias is baked into call numbers, not just attitudes.
- **Professional penalty structure**: historians of science "may openly class and
  dismiss the bulk of Islamic knowing and doing as quaint native savagery without
  professional penalty"; specialists are "pressed into religionizing the heavily
  policed evidence or (more prudently) ignoring it altogether" (*Meanings*).
- **Archive-side suppression**: key manuscript collections treated "as dirty state
  secrets or deserving targets of salafist and ethnonationalist bookburning ire"
  (*Meanings*).
- **The Ibn Turka case as proof** (*Prologue* n.7): "most of the handful of modern
  accounts of Ibn Turka still reflexively paint him as a generic Shiʿi Sufi mystic —
  exactly the charges he vehemently defended himself against… and take no notice of
  the Mafāḥiṣ." His masterwork "remains on the academic Index to this day"; MK's 2012
  dissertation "was the first study to point out the utterly obvious."
- **Ottoman counter-evidence**: the Bayezid II palace library inventory (1502/3–4)
  contains a dedicated occult-science section large enough to warrant its own chapter
  — Gardiner's "Books on Occult Sciences" (*Treasures of Knowledge*, ch. 23; note:
  the portal's metadata misattributes this chapter to MK — it is **Noah Gardiner's**)
  — plus Burak's chapter on prayers/magic squares. The Ottoman court itself filed
  these among its treasures; the phobia is the modern field's, not the period's.
- **"Safavid" as slur** (*Prologue*): the modern "colonialist-Salafist use of
  'Safavid' as a slur" plus sanctions keeping scholarship (and tourists) from Isfahan
  — occultophobia compounding with geopolitics.
