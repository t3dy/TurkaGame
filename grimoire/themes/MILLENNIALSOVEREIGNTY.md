## The claim

Early modern Islamicate sovereignty ran on **millennial time**: rulers styled as
saint-prophet-kings whose legitimacy drew on astrology, dream, lineage and the
approaching Islamic millennium (1000 AH ≈ 1591–92 CE). The occult sciences are the
*instrumentation* of this kingship — they compute, confirm and stage the ruler's
cosmic rank.

## Fixed points (held)

- **Murad III** (notes/01): "fashioned himself a prophet-saint-king on the strength
  of personal spiritual/angelic communications," reign spanning the millennium — the
  Ottoman case in the corpus.
- **Akbar** (notes/01): "the most successful avatar of this millennial
  occult-scientific sovereignty model."
- **The apocalyptic substrate** (*OC* n.20, n.31): Fleischer's "Mediterranean
  Apocalypse: Prophecies of Empire in the Fifteenth and Sixteenth Centuries";
  Bedreddīn's "utopian Apocalypse Now with lettrist overtones."
- **Ibn Turka's counter-position** (*Prologue* n.51) — the page's crown jewel: the
  New Brethren ran an **anti-apocalypticist program**: "the world is always already
  ending because the celestial Horn that is Number will sound forever." Apocalypse
  Now as *ontology*, not schedule — defusing the deadline while keeping the charge.
  Mīr Dāmād's metatime is its Safavid heir.
- **Dynastic prognosis as service** (*OC*, CareerSim's court_dynasty): "how long will
  my house last?" is millennial sovereignty's everyday retail form.

## What it gives the game

- Turka's attested stance gives the player a **third answer** wherever prophecy is
  demanded: not the flattering number, not the refusal, but the New Brethren
  reframe — *the ending is structural, sire; the question is your house's
  mathematics, not its terminus.* court_dynasty's letter-reading option could carry
  this as its triumph text; it is also the perfect Assay tell for a
  New-Brethren-ruleset floor in v2 descent.
- The millennium itself is out of CareerSim's timeframe but inside the legacy
  horizon: `source_code` runs could name the millennial courts (Murad III, Akbar) as
  the platform's eventual operators — notes/01 licenses both names.
