# PLATES: provenance for every illustration in the game

Generated by `scripts/build_plates.py` from `game/plate_selection.json` (hand-written), `research/plates_commons.json` (the Commons licence record) and `../assets/manuscripts/registry.json`. Do not hand-edit; edit the selection file and rebuild. Tested by `tests/test_plates.py`.

Ted's rule (2026-09-28): period paintings, manuscript pages and printed sources only. No photographs of places or people, no museum-object photographs, no renders, no modern paintings. A photograph of a manuscript folio is a manuscript source. Every plate is captioned in the game as **illustrative, not a depiction of this event**, with a one-sentence `relevance` that says what the picture is and what it is not.

**39 plates, 12.2 MB in `game/img/`; 24 scenes have a plate of their own, the other 12 share their act's title card or fallbacks.** From our existing collections: 5 (2 from `games/visionary-gallery`, 2 from OCCULTIMGDB, 1 already in the registry); new from Wikimedia Commons: 34. Kinds: 23 painting, 14 manuscript, 2 printed.

## Method

1. Inventory: the 30 registry items, `games/visionary-gallery` (22 Commons folios), OCCULTIMGDB (the Islamicate items) and the portal image catalogue (rejected wholesale, see below).
2. New material came only through `imagelab/scripts/fetch_commons.py`'s rights gate, imported by `scripts/fetch_plates.py` (it reads Commons' structured licence data), plus a stricter check on top (NC, ND and unparsed licences rejected).
3. Every plate has a record in the registry, added with `research/scripts/register_asset.py add` (never hand-edited), whose sha256 the build re-checks.
4. Images are resized to at most 1600 px on the long side and about 400 KB.

**A hole found in the shared rights gate (not fixed in place, because the file is shared):** `fetch_commons.FREE_KEY` accepts any key shaped `cc-by(-sa)?(-\S*)?`, which also matches `cc-by-nc-4.0` and `cc-by-nd-4.0`. No such file was in any earlier corpus, but the gate as written would pass one. `scripts/fetch_plates.py` adds the NC/ND rejection. Its opposite failure also showed: it refuses `CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO` (key absent), which is why the Khalili al-Buni folio is out.

## What each picture is not (`relevance`, one sentence each)

- `zaf-accession-1370`: Temür's court as a Shiraz workshop painted it in 1436, four years after Ibn Turka died; it shows an accession audience of 1370, not his 1387 Samarkand and not any moment of his life.
- `isk-frontispiece-lisbon`: The opening of the book made for Iskandar Sultan, the prince this act serves, at Shiraz in 1410; a picture of his court, not of any scene in Ibn Turka's service, and the figures are not identified.
- `herat-kalila-1429-front`: A garden assembly around Shāhrukh's son Bāysunghur at Herat in 1429, three years after the 1426 hearings of this act; the prince is the man who asked Ibn Turka for a book, but this is a frontispiece to a fable book, not a trial.
- `mam-kalila-1354-burzoy`: A road picture from a Mamluk-era fable book made about seventy-five years before Ibn Turka's exile; it shows travellers on animals, not his roads and not his flight from Herat.
- `herat-kulliyat-colophon`: The kind of page where a copyist in Shāhrukh's Herat records the book's making, drawn from a historian's chronicle; it is not a colophon from any Ibn Turka manuscript.
- `astro-sufi-ophiuchus-5036`: A star-book of Ibn Turka's own last years, made for the Timurid prince who ran the Samarkand observatory, showing how that world drew and labelled the sky; it is not evidence about Ibn Turka.
- `zaf-samarkand-1394`: Samarkand as Temür's capital, painted in 1436; the event is Shāhrukh's arrival in 1394, not the removal of Ibn Turka's family in 1387.
- `isk-kaaba`: How a Timurid prince's book pictured the Hijaz sanctuary in 1410-11; Ibn Turka went there c. 1393, so this is later, made in Shiraz, and is not a record of his pilgrimage or route.
- `mam-barquq-quran-open`: The finest Cairo bookmaking of the generation before Ibn Turka arrived: probably made for Sultan Sha'ban or his mother, later endowed to a Cairo mosque by Barquq's son Faraj; it belongs to his Cairo, not to his teachers or his books.
- `mam-maqamat-prince-1334`: The nearest period picture of a Mamluk ruler holding court, painted sixty years before Barquq's majlis; it is not Barquq, and not a gathering of scholars.
- `zaf-giraffe-embassy`: Cairo and Temür's court in one picture, the axis on which Ibn Turka's own life turned; it shows an embassy, not his return home and not a dated event of 1405.
- `law-governor-rahba-1237`: A petitioner standing before a seated governor in a Baghdad manuscript of 1237, earlier and Abbasid rather than Timurid; it shows the shape of a governor's audience, not Fars and not Pīr-Muḥammad.
- `isk-gulbenkian-portrait`: Iskandar Sultan in the guise of the hero Bahram Gur, as the Commons title for this folio identifies him; the second prince of this scene, pictured by his own atelier, not a scene from Ibn Turka's service.
- `isk-astrological`: A page on the constellations from the miscellany of the prince these two commentaries are for, showing that court's taste for the sciences of the heavens; it is not a page of either commentary.
- `isk-horoscope`: The prince's own birth chart, made for a book about him; it says nothing about 1414 or his fall.
- `bulhan-leo`: A page of astrological figures from a divination book of Ibn Turka's own age, showing a neighbouring occult science; it is not lettrism and not the Mafahis.
- `zaf-shahrukh-portrait`: A court likeness of the ruler who receives the charge, commissioned by his son in 1436; it is not a Herat audience of 1422 and the Commons file is a lightly retouched detail.
- `mam-fatawi-qadikhan-13`: A working legal compendium from Mamluk Egypt, a later copy, showing what a qadi's library and its certifying notes looked like; it says nothing about the school Ibn Turka judged by or about Yazd.
- `herat-baysunghur-hunt`: The prince who asked for the book, in his own Shahnama four years after the request; it is a detail of a hunt frontispiece, not the commission or any part of the treatise.
- `herat-majma-journey-1425`: A Herat picture of the Prophet's life painted a year before this treatise, showing how the court's own historian illustrated prophetic events; it does not show the splitting of the moon.
- `mam-barquq-quran-anfal`: A sura opening from a Cairo Qur'an, where the formula this commentary explains stands in its illuminated place; it is not a lettrist or Timurid copy.
- `law-governor-merv-s23`: A hearing before a governor, in an Iraqi manuscript two centuries earlier; it pictures being made to answer, not Herat and not 1426.
- `herat-baysunghur-royal-court`: A court audience in the Herat manuscript Shāhrukh's son commissioned in 1430, four years after the hearing; it shows a Shahnama reign as the atelier imagined a court, not this hearing.
- `mam-camel-riders-or9718`: The period's picture of travelling, made a century and a half before the exile; it is not Timurid and not the road out of Herat.
- `bustan-poet-judge`: A judge's court painted in Herat in 1488, late but from the same city and craft; it pictures a hearing, not this one, and not Simnan.
- `zaf-funeral-1403`: How the Timurid court painted mourning in 1436, for a prince who died in 1403; it is a mourning picture of the age, not Ibn Turka's death or burial.
- `herat-gulistan-1427-first-page`: A dated, signed first page from the Herat royal workshop in the very years Ibn Turka's copyists worked; it is the kind of page Act V's copyist produces, not one of his.
- `law-library-basra-1237`: Readers before a wall of books in 1237: the idea of a collection to be consulted; it is not fifteen leaves and not MS Majlis 10196.
- `ijaza-cairo-adab105`: An author's own certificate of transmission for a copyist, two centuries before Ibn Turka; it is the kind of document an audition note belongs to, not the Landberg note, and the reproduction is a rough high-contrast scan.
- `print-timurbec-fr-title`: Yazdi, who attended Ibn Turka's teaching, reached Europe through this 1723 translation; a printed edition of one chronicler among others' reports of the age, and it does not mention Ibn Turka.
- `isk-shirin-garden`: A princely garden gathering from Iskandar's own book, for any scene in his courts that has no picture of its own; it illustrates Nizami, not Ibn Turka.
- `zaf-wedding-guests`: The wedding of Temür's son Jahangir, who was the father of Pīr-Muḥammad; a Timurid court scene painted in 1436, not Ibn Turka's time and, as its own historians note, a stock image of a ruler with attendants.
- `zaf-hunt-bukhara`: Temür's Transoxiana in the years just after Ibn Turka's family was taken there; a hunt of 1389 painted in 1436, not any event of his life.
- `print-timurbec-giraffe`: The printed 1723 text of Yazdi's account of the Mamluk envoys who brought a giraffe; a page of an early modern translation, not a period picture and not Ibn Turka.
- `jal-yurts`: How the Jalayirid court in Baghdad drew a nomad camp c. 1400, a road-and-camp picture from the years of Temür's wars; it is not a place Ibn Turka lived.
- `herat-kulliyat-adam-angels`: A page from the chronicle of Shāhrukh's court historian, the kind of source through which others' reports of the age reach the desk; the picture is Adam and the angels and says nothing of Ibn Turka.
- `astro-sufi-orion-5036`: Another page of the Timurid star-book of the 1430s, for the historian's desk to show what a learned manuscript of Ibn Turka's world looked like; not evidence about him.
- `sufi-ursa-major`: The oldest surviving copy of the star-book that the Timurid copy for Ulugh Beg descends from, four centuries before it; the historian's reminder of how old the science was, not evidence about Ibn Turka.
- `bulhan-sagittarius`: A second astrological figure from the same divination book, for the historian's desk; another science of the age, not evidence about Ibn Turka.

## Attribution and dating uncertainty to watch

- Commons metadata is often wrong: the Lisbon frontispiece's file page says '14th century' while its category and sister files say 1410; the Barquq Qur'an is Cairo c. 1370-75 (probably for Sultan Sha'ban), endowed by Barquq's son, not made for Barquq; the file called 'Qadi Abbasid' is a Friday preacher, and was not used as a qadi.
- Holders are stated only where a Commons page or its cited source gives one. Where it does not (the funeral double page, the Jalayirid yurt drawing, the St Petersburg Maqamat, the Met Majma' folio's accession number), the plate says so.
- The Zafarnama giraffe embassy is dated 1404 in one Commons description and October 1405 in the file name; Temur died in February 1405, so the event date is treated as uncertain.
- The Bulhan plates carry the OCCULTIMGDB catalogue's shelfmark (Bodleian MS Arab. d. 138) and its Jalayirid dating, neither of which is on the Commons page (which says 1350-1450).
- The Wellcome horoscope is the only plate needing an attribution line (CC BY 4.0); it is displayed.
- Only one plate (`ijaza-cairo-adab105`) is a rough high-contrast scan, kept because an author's own certificate is the closest honest image for an audition note.
