## Caution about kinds

- What other scholars say about Ibn Turka is `reception`; MK's own analysis, objections and conjectures are
  `scholarly_argument`; descriptions of what a work argues (Mafahis, Tamhid, R. al-Inzaliyya, hierarchies in the
  R. Shaqq-i Qamar and R. Madarij) are `work`, with MK as the reporter in the mediation.
- **Qazizada's letter to Ibn Turka (EV-1683) is `reception`, not `letter`.** The duress rule lets `letter` show what
  *he* held; this letter shows what a friend thought of him. Same for the copy reports of Afandi (EV-1655, EV-1656).
- **The R. al-Inzaliyya (EV-1669)** is dated by MK "sometime before 828/1425" and the first apology is 829/1426, but
  these pages never say when the accusations began, so I did not tag it `early_work`; it is `work` (still a held kind).
  If the game wants it as the pre-accusation witness, that needs a date for the accusations from Chapter 1.
- **Qasim-i Anvar exile (EV-1674)** is `chronicle` only loosely: MK cites Virani, a modern historian, and the
  underlying witness is not named on the page.

## Where MK disputes previous scholarship's dating or attribution

- Death dates in the titles of Corbin's chapter ("ob. 830/1427") and Lewisohn's article ("d. 830/1437") are marked
  [sic] (pdf 36 nn.60, 61); MK's own dates are 770-835/1369-1432 (21). No reason is given on those pages.
  (Lewisohn's 1437 does not even match his own 830 Hijri.)
- Aubin's early assumption of Shi'i identity is noted as later modified (86 n.125).
- The EI2 and EIr entries under "Afdal al-Din Turka" cover three different family members (37 n.66): an attribution
  problem in the reference works, not in the scholarship. MK is writing the EIr entry on Ibn Turka himself (37 n.66).
- The Tamhid's adoption as a teaching text is dated "probably in the late Qajar period" (23 n.15) and its dominance of his
  reputation to the late 13th/19th century (454). These agree, not conflict, but the first is hedged.
- Ziai's statement is called "incorrect and anachronistic" (24), Tabataba'i's ranking an injustice (454 n.13),
  Lewisohn's reading a misrepresentation (25). No page of MK's here shows a rival scholar accepting the correction.

## Discrepancies and tensions

1. **Tension inside MK.** "Even a casual survey" of the writings suggests the occult-philosopher reading (25),
   yet the study is "necessarily provisional" (44), most lettrist works are unedited (23, 37) and any neat definition of the
   contribution "must be premature" (468). Both are recorded (CL-0839, CL-0838); neither is resolved.
2. **MK relies on what he discounts.** Ch. 1's biography is built "primarily" on Judi-Ni'mati and "the apologies" (39), while the
   apologies are "hardly reflective of his primary concerns" (25). Not a contradiction (biographical facts vs held
   views) but the duress rule should be applied to MK's own chapter, too.
3. **Corbin's own text cuts against the plan's caricature.** The R. Shaqq-i Qamar is the text Corbin studies (36) and also the text whose
   hierarchy MK uses to show that Ibn Turka ranked Farabi and Suhravardi low (454 n.13). Same work, two readings.
4. **"First" to treat the lettrist thought (44) versus earlier recognition.** Bihbahani (1971), Danishpazhuh (1966) and
   Zarrinkub already saw a lettrist side (38, 24 n.17); Corbin studied a lettrist-adjacent treatise (36). MK's claim is best read
   as "first sustained analysis" (CL-0841).
5. **Plan discrepancies (`docs/PLAN_TURKA_VITA_GAME.md` §4, lines 178-186).**
   - Position A gives Corbin the formula "Peripatetic + ishraqi + Akbarian synthesis". In the dissertation that formula is
     **Cooper's**, quoted by MK (25); MK ascribes to Corbin only the "standard-issue Shi'i esotericist" assumption (23, 25) and
     says the others take it over. Cooper is REC-0051, separate from Corbin (REC-0050).
   - Position E ("Hurufi sympathizer ... the prosecution at Herat, 1427"). The pages read attach the Hurufi
     link to **Qasim-i Anvar** as the official excuse for the 1427 exile (456 n.16); the label MK quotes against Ibn Turka's own circle is
     **naw-mulhidan** (455). These pages do not report any source calling Ibn Turka himself a Hurufi. Chapter 1 may; not read.
   - Position F ("underdetermined: the surviving record is duress-shaped") is not a position MK holds. MK's caveats are about provisionality and
     completeness. Nobody on these pages argues that the question cannot be decided. REC-0063 records MK's caveats without making them a rival reading.
   - Position C is MK's construction of a "de facto consensus" (25), not a school. Zarrinkub's position is missing from
     the plan; he is on record as objecting to the Spinoza title because of Ibn Turka's lettrist concerns (24 n.17).
   - "early_work (the 'Ali verse)": the 'Ali verse is not on any page I read. The epigraph on pdf 86 is Ni'mat Allah Wali's
     verse, not Ibn Turka's. The one Shi'i-looking textual detail on these pages is the altered Ibn al-Farid line in the
     Sharh-i Nazm al-Durr (92), a `work` whose date is not given.
6. **Numbers.** The oeuvre is "over half" in manuscript (23 n.13) and "roughly half" (37-38) of 46 known works; 22 are published (37),
   so 24 of 46 are not: consistent. The count leaves out the diwan and 58 letters/fragments/notes (37 n.70), while the Munsha'at-i Turka is
   "some 50" letters (43): the 58 and the 50 are not reconciled on these pages.
7. **Fame versus memory.** The Mafahis "was the basis of his fame throughout the Timurid and Safavid periods" (26) yet by the mid-11th/17th
   century popular memory "appears" to have been a failed Sufi (453), and by the late 13th/19th century he is the Tamhid (454). Not a contradiction,
   a change of who is remembering; recorded separately (CL-0811, CL-0816).
8. **MK's uncertainty about the conjunction.** He says the Saturn-Jupiter conjunction of 767/1365 is "likely" and "not entirely sure" (21 n.8): CL-0822
   is an inferential reconstruction, `low`.
9. **Mafahis dating.** "Completed in 823/1420" (26) is MK's own statement; whether that is composition or a copy's colophon is not said on the page.
   No WRK artifact was written (outside my brief).
