## Discrepancies and uncertainties (nothing resolved silently)

1. **How many letters.** p.150 says 46 letters (50 in total with 3 unique to Majlis 10196 and 1 to Malik 4029); the list itself numbers **47** Ibn Turka letters (p.155-163) plus 2 letters to him in Majlis 10196 plus 3 in other MSS; p.155 fn.111 says "six out of 47"; p.151 says MS Sinā 316 holds **45**; p.165 says Sinā 316 has "some 40"; p.152 says MS Dānishgāh 2576 continues with "letter no. 15" and holds "the remaining 32 (out of 46)" while p.166 says "letter no. 14" and "remaining 27 (out of 40)". CL-0400 records the 46/50 count as MK's own headline figure.
2. **Letter numbering off by one.** p.164 fn.152 refers to "the same Ẓahīr al-Dīn of letter 45"; in the list he is letter 46. p.163 fn.150 dates the Qāżīzāda letter "around the time of letters 24 and 25", but letters 24 and 25 are Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn and Yūsuf Khwāja and are only loosely dated to 829/1426.
3. **Year of letter 1.** Bracketed [814]/[1411] but fn.107 (p.156): 814 or 822 (1419); MK prefers 814 by assuming chronological order. CL-0404 (the letter's own statement is CL-0403).
4. **Yaʿqūb.** p.155: he "appears to have been ... qadi of Yazd after his teacher's departure"; letters 4 and 12 (pp.156-157) recommend him as replacement qadi (12: "presumably in Isfahan"), and fn.113 says this preceded Ibn Turka's own assumption of the Yazd qadiship ca. 825/1422 after his first Herat trial. So Yaʿqūb appears as replacement in Isfahan/Fars and as qadi of Yazd; MK's text does not reconcile the two and neither do I.
5. **"Amīrzāda Shāhrukh".** MK's heading for letter 20 calls Shāhrukh Amīrzāda (a prince's title) while the summary calls him sultan and letter 19 has him emperor of Iran and Turan. Probably a heading slip; flagged, not altered.
6. **Letter 27.** fn.130: whether Fīrūzshāh had already ordered the restoration of property or is being asked to is uncertain. CL-0432 keeps both readings. The pages also do not say whether the stipend was restored (CL, attested silence).
7. **Letters 33-34** addressee: three candidates, unresolved (CL-0445, undeterminable). Letters 29-32 identifications rest on Riżā Kārkiyā dying in 829/1426 (p.161 fn.137; p.164).
8. **R. Suʾl al-Mulūk date.** Four phrasings by MK: "completed in 829/1426" (p.158 fn.124), "most probably ... written in 829/1426" (p.159), "before 829/1426" (p.173), "in or before 829/1426" (p.328). The WRK written by another agent (WRK-SUAL-AL-MULUK) has `date_kind: transcription`, with the date "before 7 Rajab 829" from the Majlis 10196 copy, and folios 172b-79a; my p.331 fn.92 cites Majlis 10196 f. 178a-b for the closing section, which falls inside those folios, and p.159 fn.127 gives f. 178b. No conflict of fact, but the two ways of dating the work should be read together. I did not write a Suʾl al-Mulūk WRK.
9. **"Third introductory treatise on lettrism"** (p.158 fn.124): MK does not say which are the first two. His list (p.173) has Ḥurūf 817, Mafāḥiṣ 823, Anjām 828, Suʾl 829 (the R. Ḥurūf and R. Anjām are named as candidates for letter 15's enclosure, p.158). Not resolved.
10. **The brief assumed letters to Ulugh Beg and a Kārkiyā block "28-34".** The pages have no letter to Ulugh Beg; 28 is to the Marʿashī ruler, 29-32 to Kārkiyā sayyids and 33-34 to an ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn.
11. **Yazdī items: seven or eight?** pp.150 and 152 say seven (eight in Sinā 316); p.169 lists eight and item 2 (Ibn Turka's own majlis lines, p.168) and item 8 (Yazdī to Ibn Turka, only in Sinā 316) are not letters about the family. Item 5's sender is "Sharaf al-Dīn Yazdī (?)".
12. **Ages and dates of Jamāl al-Dīn.** His letters are dated "before 867/1463", decades after Ibn Turka's death (p.168); MK notes he may be the son of a different Turka (fn.166). Included as family evidence only, at low weight.
13. **Ordering caveats.** MK himself warns the order is not rigorous, that letter 2 is out of place (p.155 fn.108), that many letters could be from later years, and that most lack headers (pp.153-154). All dates on letters 2-47 are conjectures except where a letter says otherwise.
14. **Duress note.** Everything in `letter` evidence here can show what he *held*, but the plea letters (18, 19, 25-28, 33-34) are written by a supplicant and their mediation says so. MK himself reads the late greetings (35-47) as network management (CL-0405, psychological reconstruction, low).

## Political divination in the Suʾl al-Mulūk (pp.328-331)

Evidence EV-0891..EV-0903. Claims CL-0460..CL-0466: written at Bāysunghur's request, introductory, broader than other lettrist treatises; the only minor lettrist treatise with pointedly political content; closing prediction from Shāh Rukh's name (R 200 + Kh 600 = 800) that the state lasts through the 9th Islamic century; the prediction was well known later (Davānī); the closing section's methods (Ibn Barrajān and Ibn ʿArabī's calculation for Jerusalem 583/1187, Ibn Khallikān's earthquake example, Ḥamuvayī's cryptic method, dismissal of Ascendant-from-name); Timurid elite interest. I did not write what it "does for a prince" beyond what MK says (the closing computation is presented, in MK's translation, as an illustration that suggests the perdurance of the ascendant state); any further reading, for instance that it flatters the dynasty, would be my own and is not asserted.
